# Crime City

A GTA-style online city where every player lives a role: president, police, bounty hunter, gang member, business owner or entertainer. See the game design document for the full vision.

This repo holds **prototype 0.1**: a single-player 3D browser build of the core gang member vs. police loop.

## Play it locally

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Then open the address Vite prints (usually http://localhost:5173). Desktop browser with keyboard and mouse.

## What's in 0.1

- A 1.6 km city laid out like a real one (the plan is in src/worldplan.js, built in 3D by src/city.js; the design notes are in reports/City and highway design for games.md). Districts each have their own street pattern and look (src/districts.js pins the special places, stores, banks, homes and starting turf to spots on the map):
  - Midtown (north-west and north): a straight grid in the west, a grid turned 25 degrees in the east; mid-rise blocks, the hospital, the gun shop
  - Central Park: a 380 × 200 m park in the middle of town, with smaller parks and squares in most districts (Liberty Square, your crew's hangout, is next to it in Downtown)
  - Downtown (middle and east): a tight grid of towers cut by Grand Ave, a diagonal running from Market St up to the Government District's plaza
  - Government District (north-east): stone civic buildings with columns, City Hall (domed) on the plaza and the police station
  - Financial District (south): the old core, with irregular blocks and streets that bend and stop short
  - The Projects (west): superblocks with cul-de-sacs, long low brick buildings and a basketball court, the Southside Kings' corner
  - The Strip (south-west): clubs with neon, cut by curving Ocean Blvd
  - The Docks (south and on the water): warehouses, shipping containers, piers out into the sea, the chop shop
  - The Heights (east, outside the ring): houses with yards on a hill, winding contour roads up to Summit Park
  - Out past the ring: wooded hills, a quarry, a junkyard, a lookout, beaches along the sea and the bay
  Arterials (Park Ave, Midway Blvd, Market St, Central Ave, Grand Ave) are wide with parking lanes; collectors and local streets are narrower; hill roads have no sidewalks. Districts show as different shades on the radar and map, with faint names on the full map. Traffic and pedestrians fill the streets around you (cars more than 380 m away and people more than 300 m away are replaced nearer). Static geometry is merged by material per map chunk to keep draw calls down, trees are instanced, and collision checks use a spatial grid (buildings at any angle use rotated boxes)
- Buildings you can change (src/city.js, src/buildingedits.js): every building has a record and a stable id, "<block key>#<n>" (the n-th built on that block; the city is built the same way every time, and an edit doesn't change anyone else's id). To find one, stand next to it and run `__game.city.buildingAt()` in the browser console (or `buildingAt(x, z)`): it gives the nearest within 15 m, with its id, center, size, angle and height. Two ways to change it:
  - For good: an entry in `BUILDING_EDITS` (src/buildingedits.js), e.g. `'b947_803#1': { remove: true }` or `{ dx: 3, h: 40, turn: 0.2 }` (also x / z absolute, w / d / h, ang). It's applied as the city's built, before anything is placed against the building (shop signs and doors, trees, graffiti, turf tags), so they all follow it, and it costs nothing while you play. A removed building leaves its space empty.
  - Live, to try it out: `__game.city.editBuilding(id, { dx: 4, h: 30 })` and `__game.city.removeBuilding(id)`. These rewrite just that building's slice of the merged city mesh in place (no re-merge; well under a millisecond) along with what's attached to it (a strip club's neon, a house's roof), and update its collision box, so you, the cops, bullets and the camera all follow at once. Things placed against it at startup (signs, graffiti, trees) stay where they were until the edit goes in the file.
  - The gun shops' buildings are their rooms (src/interiors.js): move a gun shop by its block in GUN_SHOP_BLOCKS (src/districts.js), not here. Under the hood: city.js's Batch hands back a handle for every piece it merges (its mesh, first vertex, vertex count), and Batch.rewrite puts new geometry with the same vertex count in its place, or collapses it.
- Day and night (src/daynight.js): a full day is 48 real minutes (one real second is half a game minute); sessions start at 9:00 AM. The sky, fog, sun (moonlight at night) and ambient light follow the clock, with a warm glow at dawn and dusk; after dark building windows light up and the street lights come on (soft pools of light on the road). Every window on every building has a room behind it (src/winrooms.js, interior mapping in the buildings' shader: no extra geometry): walls, floor, ceiling, a sofa or desk and a picture, a door in a side wall, blinds in some, each room different and seen in depth, shifting as you move past; the glass reflects more of the sky the flatter you look at it. By day they're dim; after dark the lit ones glow warm and the rest go dark. Far off they blend into an even tone (no shimmer).
- Streets (src/streets.js): junctions of any shape. Traffic lights where main roads cross and at the busy junctions of Downtown, the Financial and Government districts and the Strip; elsewhere stop signs (a minor road meeting a bigger one stops, the main road runs through; where equal roads meet, everyone stops); bends in a road have nothing. White stop lines on every approach that stops, set back past the widest road crossing it, and street lights along every sidewalk. Signals split each junction's arms into two phases by direction (green 10 s, yellow 2.5 s, all-red 1 s), staggered junction to junction. Traffic stops at the line on red, goes through a yellow only when it's too close to stop, and at stop signs comes to a full stop and goes in the order cars arrived once the junction is clear. Pedestrians cross at the crosswalks (worked out for every arm of every junction) and wait on the curb for the walk phase (they only start across with 6+ seconds of green left, and hurry across); cars stop for anyone in front of them. Police, gang cars and backup ignore the rules. Poles and posts are solid
- Clock: top right of the HUD, e.g. "Tue 9:42 PM · Day 3" (the day count is saved; day 1 is a Monday)
- Taxis and buses (src/travel.js): fast travel with a quick cut scene (you get in, it pulls away, a title card with where you're going, the time and the fare, then you're dropped at the curb). The clock moves on by however long the drive would take. Nobody gives you a ride while you're wanted.
  - Taxi: walk up to a taxi on the street and press USE / E to hail it; it pulls over while you pick your map waypoint or a place (home, the hangout, the gun shop, the garage, the nearest bank, the chop shop, the hospital, any district). $20 plus $1 per block (bank first, then cash).
  - Bus: at any of the 23 bus stops, press USE / E and pick any stop on either route ($5, no waiting). The ★ stop is the one closest to your waypoint.
- Freeways (src/highway.js, layout in src/fwyplan.js): a ring around the city core (3 lanes each way, smooth 170 m corners) and a crosstown north-south through the middle (2 lanes each way), 7.5 m up on pillars, with a median barrier, shoulders, lane lines, guard rails and a 65 mph limit (the speedometer shows it). Ring Rd and Crosstown Rd run underneath, so streets cross under them anywhere. You always veer right onto a ramp and never cross traffic to reach one: the eight interchanges are partial cloverleaves (from the cross street every way on is a right turn — a diagonal ramp on the near side, or under the freeway and right into a loop on the far side — and every way off leaves from the right-hand lane down a deceleration lane to a T-junction with lights). Ramps climb at 6–9%, run alongside on an acceleration lane, and merge from the right. Where the crosstown meets the ring at each end, a two-loop T: the crosstown's right lane curves onto the near side of the ring; its left lane crosses over the ring on a 14 m bridge and loops round onto the far side (and the same backwards), so every move there exits right and merges from the right too. Green signs over the lanes 1/8 mile before each exit and at it, and at the curb at each entrance. Anything up there rides the surface nearest its own height (on the bridge you stay on the bridge; under it you stay under), so freeways, ramps and bridges stack. Traffic (lanes, lane changes, ramps one way, speed by the curve ahead), police chases and the GPS (which prefers the freeway for long trips and draws the route up on the decks) use them; they show in amber on the radar and the map. Police don't get out of their cars up there, and nobody walks up except you
: headlights and taillights come on after dark; each headlight throws its own soft beam straight ahead on the road (about as wide as the lamp where it starts, spreading to about 4 m and fading out about 10 m on); brake lights show whenever a driver brakes or holds the car still; delivery trucks blink their hazards when double-parked. A police car with its lights going washes the street around it red and blue (strongest at night). Empty cars stay dark
- Daily rhythms (src/rhythm.js): how many cars and people are out depends on the hour and the district. Morning rush (7–9 AM) and evening rush (4:30–6:30 PM) are the busiest times; the Financial and Government districts fill up in working hours and empty out at night; the Strip comes alive after dark (packed 10 PM–2 AM, about 1.8× the daytime crowd); the Heights stay quiet; around 4 AM the whole city is close to empty (about 10 people and 10 cars around you). The vehicle mix changes too: taxis Downtown and on the Strip, box trucks at the Docks and in working hours, sports cars in the Heights and on the Strip at night
- Streets are 16 m wide: a lane each way plus a parking shoulder each side (marked with a solid white line), so parked cars sit clear of the traffic
- Vehicle models (src/carmodels.js): every type is built from its side profile, not boxes. A real body shape (nose, hood, the windshield's slope, roof, trunk or fastback, cut-out wheel arches, shoulders that round in toward the top), tinted see-through glass (windshield, back glass, side windows; the front ones in the doors, so they swing open with them), pillars, black window trim, mirrors, bumpers, a grille, license plates, an exhaust, and wheels with a tire, a five-spoke rim and a hub (you see them turn). The cabin is open (the body dips to the floor between the windshield and the back glass, with real side walls behind the front doors), so through the glass or an open door you see the whole inside, lit softly from within (dimmer after dark): a dashboard with a padded top sloping up to the glass, a gauge hood over two dials with their needles, air vents with slats at the ends and in the middle, the radio (its blue display, volume and tuning knobs, preset buttons), a red hazard button, climate knobs and the glovebox; a steering wheel with a grippy rim, three spokes and a badge on the airbag hub, on its column with the stalks; gas and brake pedals; the console with the shifter on its plate, cupholders, the handbrake and an armrest; bucket seats with side bolsters, a lighter insert down the middle and headrests on posts, on rails; a back bench with three headrests and the parcel shelf with its speakers (four-doors); carpet over a center tunnel with floor mats; door cards inside the doors (fabric insert, armrest, handle, window switches, a speaker) and on the sides behind them; the headliner, dome light, rear-view mirror and sun visors. Every interior has its own colors. Each type its own: the sedan (four doors), the sports car (a low wedge, a fastback, a rear wing, two seats, the driver laid back), the police cruiser (black with white doors, a light bar on a roof rack), the taxi (tan inside, its roof sign and a checker band), the van (a short hood, a glass cab, a solid cargo body behind a bulkhead, the rear doors' seam), the SWAT truck (higher, armored, black), the box truck (a cab with its own hood, the box on a frame, fenders over the back wheels) and the city bus (a glass band all round with pillars, doors on the curb side, a destination sign, and inside, rows of seats in pairs down both sides, the driver's seat up front and grab poles). Whoever's driving shows through the glass, sitting in the seat with hands on the wheel, thighs along the cushion and shins down to the pedals, feet on the floor (never through it): you in your colors, cops in uniform (SWAT in black), anyone else in their own clothes; nobody when it's parked or burnt out. Each type's model is built once and shared by every car of that type (the body in one material per color, everything else in one vertex-colored material), so a car is fewer draw calls than the old boxy ones; parked cars far off (parking.js) are the same shapes, one pair of instanced meshes per type, without the insides you couldn't see anyway.
- Vehicles on the road: sedans, sports cars, vans, taxis (yellow, roof sign), box trucks (which sometimes double-park mid-block with their hazards on to make a drop; traffic goes around them) and city buses
- Buses (src/transit.js): two routes loop around the middle of the city with a bus shelter every third street (23 stops: roof, glass back wall, bench, BUS sign at the curb). Buses obey the lights, stop at each shelter for a few seconds, and people wait at the stops, get on and get off. Long vehicles stop at the line by their front bumper
- Parked cars (src/parking.js): about 1,600 cars along the curbs, more in the residential parts of town (the Heights, Midtown, the Projects, the Strip) and fewer in the business districts and at the Docks, none near the corners, bus stops or spawn points. Far away they're cheap stand-ins (three instanced meshes for the whole city); within 28 m each one becomes a real car you can hit, push, shoot or steal (a car alarm goes off about a third of the time: a small Car theft charge). A car that gets moved or wrecked leaves an empty space that fills again later
- Street props (src/props.js): a fire hydrant on every block (hit one with a car and it breaks off and sprays water about 6 m up for half a minute), trash cans near the corners (more in the busy districts), benches mid-block in Midtown, Downtown, the Heights and around the parks. Cars knock them over (they slow you down; hydrants hurt a little); you can't walk through them; they're put back once you're well away. Litter on the sidewalks and in the gutters and graffiti tags on the walls in the poorer parts of town (the Projects most, then the Docks, the Strip and Midtown; none in the Heights or the Financial District)
- Courts, fields and parks (src/court.js, src/hoops.js, src/field.js, src/football.js, src/playground.js): fenced basketball courts on Ledger Ave and Market St, Harbor Field (football practice with fans in the bleachers), and Market St Park's playground and statue plaza, each with a fenced lot. How many people turn out depends on the hour and how safe the area is (src/safety.js)
- Pickup basketball (src/streetball.js), played like NBA 2K's Blacktop: walk onto a court where people are shooting around and ask if they want to play (E / D-pad ↑ / USE: what you say changes each time — "Y'all wanna play?", "Who got next?", "Can I get in?"...). One of them answers ("Yeah! What you wanna play?", "Bet. What we playing?"...) and the court menu comes up (src/courtmenu.js; the game waits while it's open): "SMOKE'S RUN" with the court's name and your cash; CHOOSE YOUR GAME — 1 ON 1 vs. him (his overall rating, 60–90, and the length) or 33 with everybody at that basket (the chosen one is boxed); HOUSE RULES — Wager ‹ $100 › (they match it) and Play to ‹ 11 › (11, 15 or 21, for the one-on-one; both remembered); START GAME · $100; or Watch courtside. Controller: D-pad ↑↓ to move, ◀ ▶ to change, ✕ to pick, ○ to back out; keyboard: arrows / WASD, Enter / E, Esc; mouse and phone: tap a game, tap the ‹ › arrows, tap START. 1s and 2s; after a defensive rebound or a steal take it back past the arc; a foul on a made shot is an and-one. Check-ups, like real pickup ball: at the start, after every bucket (make it, take it: the scorer keeps the ball) and after a foul or stoppage you're both put back at the top of the key face to face (the ball handler just past the arc, the defender a step in front). The ball goes to the defender and back on its own (no button) and then it's live. Crouch (L3), the test car (R3), the inventory strip and weapon swaps are off while you play (shooting around too), so the sticks are only for the game. In a one-on-one, 33 or the 3-point contest (and their intros) texts and City News / Crime Reports don't pop up over the game and the phone hint is hidden (src/hud.js quiet): they still land in the phone, unread, to read after (shooting around on your own they pop up as usual). Controls follow 2K's layout on the controller: □ hold and let go at the top of the shot meter to shoot (a tap is a pump fake). The shot depends on how you're moving: driving at the rim from 5 m or closer it's a layup (the gather, then two steps, up off the second; the gather isn't a step, as in the NBA rule), sprinting at it from 3.8 m or closer a dunk (gather and two steps from over 2.5 m, one step from 1.2–2.5 m, a two-foot jump stop inside 1.2 m), driving in and pulling the left stick back away from the rim a floater, anything else a jumper. R2 / ✕ sprint; the right stick is the Pro Stick (flick left/right crossover, up hesitation, hold up to size up, down step-back, down-diagonal spin, up-diagonal behind the back; a move during a layup is a euro step), and the camera looks down the court on its own. On defense: L2 intense D (face him and slide; drive into you and he's cut off; box out on a shot), □ steal (best while he's mid-move; reach twice or from behind and it's a foul), △ block / jump for a rebound. A block is live for the whole jump: go up a beat early (or as he rises) and it's tried when his shot gets near the release while you're still in the air (one try per jump), so the phone's BLOCK button doesn't need frame-perfect timing. Keyboard: hold click / Space shoot, Shift sprint, J / L crossover, I hesi (hold: size-up), K step-back, U / O spin, B / N behind the back, right-click intense D, click / R steal, F block. Before a one-on-one, the matchup (src/matchup.js): a short cinematic (about 4 s, the game waits) with the two of you squared up at the top of the key and the camera swinging slowly round you, letterboxed: 1V1 MATCHUP, YOU vs him with your overalls (yours: the average of your handle and defense; each with its bar, handle and defense), what it's to and what's on it (FIRST TO 11 · $100 EACH), and a line from him; any tap, ✕, Space, Enter or Esc skips it, then the check-up. Every make, miss and turnover resets the play (a one-on-one): make it, take it (the defender goes and gets it from under the net and checks it up to the scorer); a miss is the other one's ball, no live rebounds (the shooter gets it and checks it up to him); a loose ball taken by the other one (a steal, a block, a poke) is a turnover and the same. You do your part on your own (jog to the ball, dribble it to the free-throw line or walk out past the arc; SHOOT / ✕ / E skips straight to the check); he does his the same way. A poke or block the ball handler gets back himself is play on. GUARD (L2 / right-click; the phone's GUARD button, on defense only) is the hold to stay in front: in close you're drawn to the spot between him and the basket and keep pace with him, stronger with a better defense rating, unless you push the other way to where he's going or he's just beaten you. Every player has a defense rating (55-99): his comes from his overall and style and shows at the start; yours starts at 72 and grows with stops (his misses, your takeaways) and is saved. It also sets how often moves beat you in GUARD, and how quick his slide and his recovery are when he guards you (the same scale both ways). The moves list (src/moveslist.js): every move for the game you're in, written for what you're playing on (phone, controller or keyboard); it pauses the game while it's open. Open it with ? (top of the screen, right of QUIT, on a phone), Create on the controller or Tab on the keyboard; close it the same way, ○ / Esc, or a tap outside it. Football has its own list. Phone: with the ball, SHOOT and PASS, and the dribble moves are gestures on the right side of the screen (tap: hesitation; swipe left / right: crossover that way; up: spin; down: step-back; all in the moves list); Shooting around, PASS passes (hold to lock on) and OOP tosses the alley-oop; in a one-on-one there's nobody to pass to, so the button is OOP. An alley-oop's takeoff is within about 2 m of where the ball comes down (you and him): tossed from the arc you have to run in and get there in time, or it comes down without you. On defense: STEAL, △, D and HANDS (D only on defense; with the ball the back-pedal is automatic). No USE button in a pickup game on a phone: QUIT puts the ball down / leaves. The shot meter: the green window depends on the shot (a three from the arc to 8 m: ~92 ms; closer jumpers wider, up to ~170 ms; layups 200 ms; floaters 120 ms; past 8 m it shrinks fast: ~40 ms at 10 m, ~9 ms at 14 m, a couple of ms from three-quarter court, where even a green can miss); an EXCELLENT (green) release always goes in from 14 m or closer; he's on the same windows (his timing is off by an amount set by his rating, ~90 ms at 60 OVR to ~60 ms at 90, and it's green when it lands inside the window); the green window shrinks when he's in your face, on the run, from deep and when you're tired; shot feedback says how early or late you were and how open (Wide Open → Heavily Contested). Energy drains with sprinting and moves and comes back standing still (below about 45% the colors drain out and a red vignette closes in, pulsing when you're nearly empty); tired players are slower, shoot worse and get beaten off the dribble. Dunks are animated: you glide in, rise to the rim and throw it down (two-hand, one-hand, windmill or tomahawk), hang on the rim after a make and drop; so does he, with the same gather-and-steps footwork on his layups and dunks. Your energy lasts about 50% longer than his. Alley-oops are one move: △ (F, the △ button) from 3–9.5 m tosses it off the backboard to yourself, and it comes off the glass to a spot about a meter out from the rim on the side your run is taking you (left wing, right wing or down the middle). Keep running at it: once you're in reach the gather, steps and leap play out on their own, timed so the top of the leap meets the ball, and the shot meter shows beside you in the air. Press SHOOT (□ / click / Space) at the top: in the green you catch it and throw it down (two-hand or windmill coming straight down the middle, one-hand or tomahawk from the side); a little early or late you catch it but only lay it in; way off, or no press, it comes off your hands. The window is tighter with him at the spot. In a one-on-one he runs to the spot once it's off the glass and can go up and swat it away or pick it off. He throws oops to himself too (the athletic ones, now and then on a drive), with his timing set by his rating, and you can break his up: △ near the ball as it comes down. Shooting around, cut to the rim after you pass and the man you passed to lobs it up for you (the same timed catch). Every dunk now finishes from the side you came in on instead of always straight in front of the rim. A dunk shakes the camera a little. A step-back keeps you facing the basket. Hold L2 (right-click) with the ball and you stay square to the basket whichever way you move: backing up is a low backward shuffle, sideways a side-step (slower, no sprint); without it, going away from the basket you turn and go (on a phone the back-pedal is automatic). On defense L2 keeps your back to the basket, square to the play, and retreating toward the rim is the same shuffle. He back-pedals the same way: backing it out past the arc facing the basket, retreating on D facing you. And he faces up like your L2: with you within 3.5 m (or backing it out) he stays square to the basket whichever way he moves (backing up at 65%, sideways at 80%); driving, or walking to a spot with nobody near, he turns and goes. Shoot a jumper drifting away from the basket (or with the stick pulled back when you're not driving, or off a step-back) and it's a fadeaway: you drift back and lean through the jump; the defender's contest counts a level less, the green window is 10% smaller. His step-backs push off and hop back smoothly, and he fades away (drifting back and leaning through the jump) off a step-back or sometimes when you're right on him: a little harder to green, but your contest counts for less. After a shot he finishes his jump before he moves. Your jumper rises to the top of the jump right at the perfect release (a quick jump, arms up fast) (hold it and you're coming down). Shot form (both of you): a jumper gathers the ball up from the dribble into both hands at the chest with a dip at the knees, rises to a set point above the forehead (elbow under the ball, wrist cocked, the guide hand on its side), extends to the release at the top and snaps the wrist through (a held follow-through while the guide hand drops), then lands soft on bent knees. A layup reaches up one-handed off the ball's hand, the knee on that side driving up, the off arm out protecting it; a floater is an earlier, softer one-hand push. The ball sits in the hands the whole way. You shoot jumpers right-handed; he's sometimes a lefty. In the air after a shot the stick can't turn you, and after a fadeaway you land and back-pedal a step, still facing the basket, before you can turn and go (he does the same). The shot meter stands upright beside your player, and your energy bar follows under your feet. On the controller and on a phone the camera aims from you at the basket (a spot just behind the rim), so it's in view from the corners too (on a phone you can still drag to look around; it swings back to the basket about a second after you let go). Turned sideways, a ballhandler switches it to the outside hand, away from the defender (or from the basket side when nobody's on him); he does too. Ball handling: every player has a handle rating (55–99). It sets how far the dribble moves carry (a 60 goes about 70% as far as a 90) and how well they work (beating a man, not getting stripped). His comes from his overall and style (slashers higher, shooters lower) and shows when the game starts; yours starts at 72, grows as your moves beat people in games (more for an ankle breaker), is saved, and shows on the shootaround board. Crossovers: a sideways flick of the right stick is a quick crossover right in front of you (a quick shuffle to that side, ~0.9 m at a 90 handle, chainable back to back); with R2 held it's the big burst crossover. Flicking toward the hand the ball's already in is a hesitation (a hard step and a dip on the spot; with R2 a hop out to the ball's side, ~1.2 m at a 90 handle, staying square). Any other move can cut a hesitation short (not another hesitation). Combos: flick during a move and the next one fires the moment it ends (a quick crossover can be cut a little past halfway), so left-right is a double crossover; He strings double crossovers together too. The spin is a full 360° turn toward the side the ball's on (the ball hand leading), the ball tucked at the hip and pulled round on that hand, the free elbow out; about two-thirds of the way round it's let go and bounced once across in front into the other hand (never handed over: that'd be a travel), coming out squared up. Every joint, the height and the facing ease into each new pose (~0.1 s), so moves, stances and shots blend instead of snapping, for both of you. After a crossover you square back up to the basket unless the left stick is pointing somewhere. He plays by the same rules: quick crossovers he strings together, big burst ones, side hesitations, squaring up after. The dribbling stance (both of you): knees bent and low standing still, the off arm up in front guarding the ball. Crossovers (and between the legs, behind the back) don't turn you: you shuffle out sideways to the new hand's side, lead leg reaching, leaning into it. A hesitation is a hard step out to the ball's side, the lead leg wide and bent, the weight over it. On defense (both of you): a wide base, knees bent deep, one hand up to bother the shot and one low and out to poke at the ball; sliding sideways the feet push apart and come together without crossing, leaning into the slide; backing up is the backward shuffle. He always slides facing you; you slide while holding L2 (without it you run to recover, in your stance when you stop). Between the legs: hold L2 and flick the right stick sideways (keyboard H, or right-click with J / L). Turned sideways with the ball on the trouble's side (the defender close by, otherwise the basket), a ballhandler switches hands on his own: if the trouble is still out in front (he turned toward it) it goes behind the back; if it's behind him (he turned away) a crossover in front or between the legs. Steals and blocks knock the ball loose instead of handing it over: a poke rolls it a couple of meters, mostly toward the hand that poked it (whoever gets there first has it, and the one who lost it can't grab it straight back; the defender's side has to take it back past the arc); a block swats it away from the blocker's hand. Chase-down blocks: beaten on a drive, run him down from behind and press △ as he goes up for a layup or dunk (a longer reach than a normal block, rarely a foul) and you pin it on the glass; he chases you down the same way when you beat him. The layup's hand follows the left stick: holding it to the right of the rim it's a right-handed layup, to the left a left-handed one, straight at it the hand the ball's in (his follows the side he's heading to). Layups go off the glass (up to the square on your side, then down into the rim) unless you're coming straight down the middle at the basket (within about 20°), where they go straight in; his too. Supply is citywide per drug (the plugs) plus local (rival gangs dealing on their turf): raids cut a drug's supply and make plugs charge more; plugs can be killed (that drug dries up until a new plug takes over, inheriting your tab); each rival gang has a kingpin (♛) at its home base, and killing one dries up their corners; your big runs flood the area and push prices down. Approval drifts toward 65% in a quiet city and falls as crime climbs; arrests, drug raids and street sweeps help, suspects escaping hurts. Crackdowns and the war on drugs read as strong when crime is high and heavy-handed when it's quiet. Big swings make City News; the panel shows the rating, the crime level and what's driving it
- War on drugs (pause menu → President's orders): Hands off / Tough on drugs / All-out war. Each step scares off buyers (demand −15%), seizes supply (−20%, so prices and plug costs climb), makes raids on pickups likelier and puts more undercover cops among buyers; all-out war adds street sweeps that clear a district's corners every few minutes
- Selling on rival turf (src/turfrisk.js): their buyers are loyal (pay 25% less, come less often) unless that gang's kingpin is dead (then their buyers come to you and pay 25% more). Each sale can get you noticed — more near their people, on their home block, and the more you sell on one block: first a warning text, then (within 3 minutes) their crew opens fire or drives over to find you. Sometimes one of their soldiers walks up for a 30% cut of what you made there: pay (E / ✕) and you can sell there for 5 minutes, refuse (Q / ○) and they come at you — drop and they take everything you're holding. ## Playing on a phone

Open the game link on your phone and turn it sideways. Touch controls switch on at the first touch (src/touch.js); they work like a virtual PS5 controller, so every prompt, menu and the phone work the same way.
- Left thumb: a joystick that appears wherever you touch the left side. On foot it moves you (push it all the way out to sprint); in a car it steers, and up/down is gas/brake. Driving is tuned for thumbs: steering is gentle near the center (full lock at the edge of a wider sideways throw), turns less at speed and moves the wheel smoothly; push up past a third for gas and it stays on while you steer until you pull back below the middle (same for the brake); the camera swings back behind the car sooner.
- Right thumb: drag anywhere on the right side to look around. Drag off FIRE to aim while shooting. AIM is a toggle (tap on, tap off) and gets the controller's aim assist.
- USE is a phone's one contextual button: it says what it'll do for whatever you're next to and lights up yellow — PICK UP, DROP OFF, PAY, BUY, SELL, ROB, SWAP, BANK, ATM, SHOP, MOD, TAXI, BUS, RECRUIT, TAG, CLAIM, TALK (asking a court to play), JOIN / START (dice), ENTER (a car, or your safehouse), JACK (a car with someone in it) — and in a car it's EXIT. (So there's no △, □ or ○ on foot, and no △ in the car.)
- The phone's buttons use simple icons where they're things: the phone, the map, the test car up top, and the GUN button showing what's in your hands (a fist, pistol, SMG, rifle or shotgun; an amber ring runs round it while you reload). On foot the right-hand buttons sit in an arc round FIRE (bottom right): AIM to its left, the GUN button up-left, DUCK above, USE further out; ✕ / ○ (yes / no) appear to the left only while you're being asked. On short screens the arc tightens; on narrow ones the top row shifts right to clear the info in the corner.
- On foot: FIRE, AIM, DUCK, the GUN button (tap to swap weapons, hold to reload — it reads RELOAD while you do — swipe up on it for the inventory strip, tap again to close it) and USE (above). With your fists out, FIRE reads PUNCH and throws one (a melee weapon: SWING). ✕ / ○ show up as yes / no only while someone's asking (a buyer's offer, a corner tax, a ticket; in a car too).
- Driving: GAS, BRAKE, HAND BRAKE, △ exit, ✕ / ○ for prompts (tickets, deals), USE.
- Top row: pause, 📱 phone, MAP, CAR (test car while that testing switch is on).
- Phone, shop menus and the inventory strip: a D-pad plus ✕ / ○ on the left. Rows can be tapped too: the first tap selects (so you can read the details), the second buys or opens.
- Map: drag to pan (when zoomed in), pinch to zoom, tap to drop a waypoint; buttons for zoom, legend, heat map and close.
- Phones get "Phone" graphics automatically (src/quality.js): lower render resolution, no antialiasing, cheaper and shorter shadows, a shorter view distance (fog closes in sooner), 60% of the people, 70% of the traffic and fewer parked cars drawn far away. Switch between Phone and Full in the pause menu (saved), or add `?quality=phone` / `?quality=full` to the address. `?touch` forces the touch controls on.
- The HUD, phone, shops, map, pause menu and title screen are laid out for small landscape screens; in portrait you're asked to turn the phone. Android goes full screen when you tap PLAY (on iPhone, use Share → Add to Home Screen for full screen).

## Street layout clean-up
Each district lays out its own street grid, so worldplan.js tidies up where they meet (`tidyStreets`): a street that mostly runs right alongside a bigger one is dropped (no overlapping pavement), and a street ending a few meters from an existing junction is moved onto it (one crossing instead of a jog). Stop signs and signal poles stand on the curb clear of every road, following the street round its bends; where there's no clear curb (two junctions very close, at the interchanges) the pole is left out.

## Merging and the camera under the freeway
- Entrances: every acceleration lane runs on alongside the freeway (round the ring's corners too) for up to 150 m, then tapers into the right lane over up to 70 m; it ends before the next exit. Where the next exit is close (the ring's interchanges are ~400 m apart), it's an auxiliary lane that runs straight on into that exit: merge left anywhere along it, or stay in it to get off. AI cars can move over anywhere along these lanes too (fwyplan.js `addTails`, highway.js).
- Under the freeway, the camera stays below the deck (it eases down just before you drive under and back up after), so the deck never blocks the view (main.js `updateCamera`, highway.js `ceilingAbove`).

## Interchange entrances
- The ramps are laid out for the real cross streets (all avenues, 20 m curb to curb): exits come down square to the curb, the loop entrances peel off the curb lane, the diagonal entrances leave from the curb. The short links between the middle of the street and each ramp end are for traffic only (inside the street, nothing drawn), so there are no paved blobs at the ramp ends.
- Outside the ring the ground is levelled over the interchange land and eases back into the hills around it, so the ramps and the street meet at the same height.
- Roads on the hills: the ground under a road takes its height from the nearest piece of that road (it used to take the first piece within reach, which buried downhill stretches under the grass).
- Entrance signs are small green signs on a pole by the curb ("RING HWY EAST ↗"), on the ground there, moved clear of roads and ramps. Street lights and stop/signal poles are never placed in a road, on a ramp, or in the ramp land.
- The diagonal entrances peel off the curb lane in a smooth right-hand curve (no square turn), well clear of the exit opposite. Traffic only turns onto an entrance from the side where it's a right turn (`approach` on the ramp-end node; traffic.js `pickNext`). Ramp ends ride a little above the street and grass where they meet, and the camera's near plane is 0.3 m (sharper depth far off), so the grass doesn't flicker through.

## Interchange editor (testing tool)
Pause → "Interchange editor" (switch: `RAMP_EDITOR` in src/config.js). A top-down view of one side of one interchange (◀ ▶ to move between them) with its three ramps: exit (red), loop entrance (green), diagonal entrance (blue). Each ramp is a smooth curve through control points: drag a dot to move it, tap a ramp to add a dot, select a dot and DELETE to remove it, UNDO, RESET. The square dot (where the ramp joins the freeway) stays put. Drag empty space to pan; pinch, the + / − buttons or the mouse wheel zoom.
- Curves: tap a dot to select it and its two curve handles appear (yellow diamonds): drag one to bend the curve there (like a drawing program's Bezier handles); SMOOTH puts that dot back to automatic. The ramps are cubic Bezier pieces between the dots.
- SNAP (on by default): drag a ramp's street end (the exit's last dot, an entrance's first) near a street and it locks into that street's curb lane, the ramp's outer edge in line with the curb and its direction along the street, so it splits off (or merges in) seamlessly; a yellow ring marks a snapped end. Traffic takes a snapped entrance from the direction it leaves the street.
- "EDITING: ALL" changes the design every interchange on that freeway shares (ring or crosstown); "THIS ONE ONLY" changes just that side (and wins over the shared design).
- SAVE & DRIVE stores the edits in the browser (`crimecity.rampedits.v1`) and reloads; the city is rebuilt from them (ramp heights follow the old profile along the new curve; the land kept clear for ramps, the street links and the entrance signs follow the ramps) and you start on the cross street facing that interchange.
- COPY shows the edits as text to send over, so they can be built in for good (fwyplan.js `EDITS` / `editableRamp`, rampeditor.js).
- The ring interchanges now use the design made in the editor (fwyplan.js `BAKED`): the loop and diagonal entrances peel off the avenue's curb lane, and the exit curves down and merges into a road alongside the freeway (on the far edge of the interchange land) that runs on to the avenue. Out of town that road is just the stretch from the exit to the avenue. The editor starts from this design; RESET goes back to it.

## Signs, signals, lights and markings
- Traffic lights stand on the far side of the junction (on the right, just past the crosswalk), where a driver waiting at the line can see them; two approaches wanting the same corner share a pole. Stop signs stand on the near right-hand curb. Poles are never in a road or on a ramp (more room on the hill roads, which are strips laid on the terrain).
- Street name signs: green blades on the corner pole of each junction (the stop sign's or the light's), one per named street, each running along the street it names (all names in one texture: one draw call).
- Street lights keep 2 m from sign and signal poles; hydrants, trash cans and benches keep clear of all of them and never stand in a road.
- Markings (center dashes, parking lines, stop lines, crosswalks) sit just above the road surface everywhere, including the hill roads (city.js `roadY`); the center line runs on round bends (it only stops short at real junctions). Hill roads have a wider flat strip either side so the hillside doesn't poke through their edges.
