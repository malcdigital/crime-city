# Crime City

A GTA-style online city where every player lives a role: president, police, bounty hunter, gang member, business owner or entertainer. See the game design document for the full vision.

This repo holds **prototype 0.1**: a single-player 3D browser build of the core gang member vs. police loop.

## Play it locally

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Then open the address Vite prints (usually http://localhost:5173). Desktop browser with keyboard and mouse.

## What's in 0.1

- A 1.6 km city laid out like a real one (the plan is in src/worldplan.js, built in 3D by src/city.js; the design notes are in reports/City and highway design for games.md). Districts each have their own street pattern and look (src/districts.js pins the special places, stores, banks, homes and starting turf to spots on the map):
  - Midtown (north-west and north): a straight grid in the west, a grid turned 25 degrees in the east; mid-rise blocks, the hospital, the gun shop
  - Central Park: a 380 × 200 m park in the middle of town, with smaller parks and squares in most districts (Liberty Square, your crew's hangout, is next to it in Downtown)
  - Downtown (middle and east): a tight grid of towers cut by Grand Ave, a diagonal running from Market St up to the Government District's plaza
  - Government District (north-east): stone civic buildings with columns, City Hall (domed) on the plaza and the police station
  - Financial District (south): the old core, with irregular blocks and streets that bend and stop short
  - The Projects (west): superblocks with cul-de-sacs, long low brick buildings and a basketball court, the Southside Kings' corner
  - The Strip (south-west): clubs with neon, cut by curving Ocean Blvd
  - The Docks (south and on the water): warehouses, shipping containers, piers out into the sea, the chop shop
  - The Heights (east, outside the ring): houses with yards on a hill, winding contour roads up to Summit Park
  - Out past the ring: wooded hills, a quarry, a junkyard, a lookout, beaches along the sea and the bay
  Arterials (Park Ave, Midway Blvd, Market St, Central Ave, Grand Ave) are wide with parking lanes; collectors and local streets are narrower; hill roads have no sidewalks. Districts show as different shades on the radar and map, with faint names on the full map. Traffic and pedestrians fill the streets around you (cars more than 380 m away and people more than 300 m away are replaced nearer). Static geometry is merged by material per map chunk to keep draw calls down, trees are instanced, and collision checks use a spatial grid (buildings at any angle use rotated boxes)
- Day and night (src/daynight.js): a full day is 48 real minutes (one real second is half a game minute); sessions start at 9:00 AM. The sky, fog, sun (moonlight at night) and ambient light follow the clock, with a warm glow at dawn and dusk; after dark building windows light up and the street lights come on (soft pools of light on the road)
- Streets (src/streets.js): junctions of any shape. Traffic lights where main roads cross and at the busy junctions of Downtown, the Financial and Government districts and the Strip; elsewhere stop signs (a minor road meeting a bigger one stops, the main road runs through; where equal roads meet, everyone stops); bends in a road have nothing. White stop lines on every approach that stops, set back past the widest road crossing it, and street lights along every sidewalk. Signals split each junction's arms into two phases by direction (green 10 s, yellow 2.5 s, all-red 1 s), staggered junction to junction. Traffic stops at the line on red, goes through a yellow only when it's too close to stop, and at stop signs comes to a full stop and goes in the order cars arrived once the junction is clear. Pedestrians cross at the crosswalks (worked out for every arm of every junction) and wait on the curb for the walk phase (they only start across with 6+ seconds of green left, and hurry across); cars stop for anyone in front of them. Police, gang cars and backup ignore the rules. Poles and posts are solid
- Clock: top right of the HUD, e.g. "Tue 9:42 PM · Day 3" (the day count is saved; day 1 is a Monday)
- Taxis and buses (src/travel.js): fast travel with a quick cut scene (you get in, it pulls away, a title card with where you're going, the time and the fare, then you're dropped at the curb). The clock moves on by however long the drive would take. Nobody gives you a ride while you're wanted.
  - Taxi: walk up to a taxi on the street and press USE / E to hail it; it pulls over while you pick your map waypoint or a place (home, the hangout, the gun shop, the garage, the nearest bank, the chop shop, the hospital, any district). $20 plus $1 per block (bank first, then cash).
  - Bus: at any of the 23 bus stops, press USE / E and pick any stop on either route ($5, no waiting). The ★ stop is the one closest to your waypoint.
- Freeways (src/highway.js, layout in src/fwyplan.js): a ring around the city core (3 lanes each way, smooth 170 m corners) and a crosstown north-south through the middle (2 lanes each way), 7.5 m up on pillars, with a median barrier, shoulders, lane lines, guard rails and a 65 mph limit (the speedometer shows it). Ring Rd and Crosstown Rd run underneath, so streets cross under them anywhere. You always veer right onto a ramp and never cross traffic to reach one: the eight interchanges are partial cloverleaves (from the cross street every way on is a right turn — a diagonal ramp on the near side, or under the freeway and right into a loop on the far side — and every way off leaves from the right-hand lane down a deceleration lane to a T-junction with lights). Ramps climb at 6–9%, run alongside on an acceleration lane, and merge from the right. Where the crosstown meets the ring at each end, a two-loop T: the crosstown's right lane curves onto the near side of the ring; its left lane crosses over the ring on a 14 m bridge and loops round onto the far side (and the same backwards), so every move there exits right and merges from the right too. Green signs over the lanes 1/8 mile before each exit and at it, and at the curb at each entrance. Anything up there rides the surface nearest its own height (on the bridge you stay on the bridge; under it you stay under), so freeways, ramps and bridges stack. Traffic (lanes, lane changes, ramps one way, speed by the curve ahead), police chases and the GPS (which prefers the freeway for long trips and draws the route up on the decks) use them; they show in amber on the radar and the map. Police don't get out of their cars up there, and nobody walks up except you
: headlights and taillights come on after dark; each headlight throws its own soft beam straight ahead on the road (about as wide as the lamp where it starts, spreading to about 4 m and fading out about 10 m on); brake lights show whenever a driver brakes or holds the car still; delivery trucks blink their hazards when double-parked. A police car with its lights going washes the street around it red and blue (strongest at night). Empty cars stay dark
- Daily rhythms (src/rhythm.js): how many cars and people are out depends on the hour and the district. Morning rush (7–9 AM) and evening rush (4:30–6:30 PM) are the busiest times; the Financial and Government districts fill up in working hours and empty out at night; the Strip comes alive after dark (packed 10 PM–2 AM, about 1.8× the daytime crowd); the Heights stay quiet; around 4 AM the whole city is close to empty (about 10 people and 10 cars around you). The vehicle mix changes too: taxis Downtown and on the Strip, box trucks at the Docks and in working hours, sports cars in the Heights and on the Strip at night
- Streets are 16 m wide: a lane each way plus a parking shoulder each side (marked with a solid white line), so parked cars sit clear of the traffic
- Vehicles on the road: sedans, sports cars, vans, taxis (yellow, roof sign), box trucks (which sometimes double-park mid-block with their hazards on to make a drop; traffic goes around them) and city buses
- Buses (src/transit.js): two routes loop around the middle of the city with a bus shelter every third street (23 stops: roof, glass back wall, bench, BUS sign at the curb). Buses obey the lights, stop at each shelter for a few seconds, and people wait at the stops, get on and get off. Long vehicles stop at the line by their front bumper
- Parked cars (src/parking.js): about 1,600 cars along the curbs, more in the residential parts of town (the Heights, Midtown, the Projects, the Strip) and fewer in the business districts and at the Docks, none near the corners, bus stops or spawn points. Far away they're cheap stand-ins (three instanced meshes for the whole city); within 28 m each one becomes a real car you can hit, push, shoot or steal (a car alarm goes off about a third of the time: a small Car theft charge). A car that gets moved or wrecked leaves an empty space that fills again later
- Street props (src/props.js): a fire hydrant on every block (hit one with a car and it breaks off and sprays water about 6 m up for half a minute), trash cans near the corners (more in the busy districts), benches mid-block in Midtown, Downtown, the Heights and around the parks. Cars knock them over (they slow you down; hydrants hurt a little); you can't walk through them; they're put back once you're well away. Litter on the sidewalks and in the gutters and graffiti tags on the walls in the poorer parts of town (the Projects most, then the Docks, the Strip and Midtown; none in the Heights or the Financial District)
- Courts, fields and parks (src/court.js, src/hoops.js, src/field.js, src/football.js, src/playground.js): fenced basketball courts on Ledger Ave and Market St, Harbor Field (football practice with fans in the bleachers), and Market St Park's playground and statue plaza, each with a fenced lot. How many people turn out depends on the hour and how safe the area is (src/safety.js)
- Pickup basketball (src/streetball.js), played like NBA 2K's Blacktop: walk onto a court where people are shooting around and ask if they want to play (E / D-pad ↑ / USE: what you say changes each time — "Y'all wanna play?", "Who got next?", "Can I get in?"...). One of them answers ("Yeah! What you wanna play?", "Bet. What we playing?"...) and the court menu comes up (src/courtmenu.js; the game waits while it's open): "SMOKE'S RUN" with the court's name and your cash; CHOOSE YOUR GAME — 1 ON 1 vs. him (his overall rating, 60–90, and the length) or 33 with everybody at that basket (the chosen one is boxed); HOUSE RULES — Wager ‹ $100 › (they match it) and Play to ‹ 11 › (11, 15 or 21, for the one-on-one; both remembered); START GAME · $100; or Watch courtside. Controller: D-pad ↑↓ to move, ◀ ▶ to change, ✕ to pick, ○ to back out; keyboard: arrows / WASD, Enter / E, Esc; mouse and phone: tap a game, tap the ‹ › arrows, tap START. 1s and 2s; after a defensive rebound or a steal take it back past the arc; a foul on a made shot is an and-one. Check-ups, like real pickup ball: at the start, after every bucket (make it, take it: the scorer keeps the ball) and after a foul or stoppage you're both put back at the top of the key face to face (the ball handler just past the arc, the defender a step in front). The ball goes to the defender and back on its own (no button) and then it's live. Crouch (L3), the test car (R3), the inventory strip and weapon swaps are off while you play (shooting around too), so the sticks are only for the game. Controls follow 2K's layout on the controller: □ hold and let go at the top of the shot meter to shoot (a tap is a pump fake). The shot depends on how you're moving: driving at the rim from 5 m or closer it's a layup (the gather, then two steps, up off the second; the gather isn't a step, as in the NBA rule), sprinting at it from 3.8 m or closer a dunk (gather and two steps from over 2.5 m, one step from 1.2–2.5 m, a two-foot jump stop inside 1.2 m), driving in and pulling the left stick back away from the rim a floater, anything else a jumper. R2 / ✕ sprint; the right stick is the Pro Stick (flick left/right crossover, up hesitation, hold up to size up, down step-back, down-diagonal spin, up-diagonal behind the back; a move during a layup is a euro step), and the camera looks down the court on its own. On defense: L2 intense D (face him and slide; drive into you and he's cut off; box out on a shot), □ steal (best while he's mid-move; reach twice or from behind and it's a foul), △ block / jump for a rebound. Keyboard: hold click / Space shoot, Shift sprint, J / L crossover, I hesi (hold: size-up), K step-back, U / O spin, B / N behind the back, right-click intense D, click / R steal, F block. Phone: SHOOT, CROSS, HESI, STEP, SPIN on offense; STEAL, △ and D on defense. The shot meter: the green window depends on the shot (a three from the arc to 8 m: ~92 ms; closer jumpers wider, up to ~170 ms; layups 200 ms; floaters 120 ms; past 8 m it shrinks fast: ~40 ms at 10 m, ~9 ms at 14 m, a couple of ms from three-quarter court, where even a green can miss); an EXCELLENT (green) release always goes in from 14 m or closer; he's on the same windows (his timing is off by an amount set by his rating, ~90 ms at 60 OVR to ~60 ms at 90, and it's green when it lands inside the window); the green window shrinks when he's in your face, on the run, from deep and when you're tired; shot feedback says how early or late you were and how open (Wide Open → Heavily Contested). Energy drains with sprinting and moves and comes back standing still (below about 45% the colors drain out and a red vignette closes in, pulsing when you're nearly empty); tired players are slower, shoot worse and get beaten off the dribble. Dunks are animated: you glide in, rise to the rim and throw it down (two-hand, one-hand, windmill or tomahawk), hang on the rim after a make and drop; so does he, with the same gather-and-steps footwork on his layups and dunks. Your energy lasts about 50% longer than his. Alley-oops are one move: △ (F, the △ button) from 3–9.5 m tosses it off the backboard to yourself, and it comes off the glass to a spot about a meter out from the rim on the side your run is taking you (left wing, right wing or down the middle). Keep running at it: once you're in reach the gather, steps and leap play out on their own, timed so the top of the leap meets the ball, and the shot meter shows beside you in the air. Press SHOOT (□ / click / Space) at the top: in the green you catch it and throw it down (two-hand or windmill coming straight down the middle, one-hand or tomahawk from the side); a little early or late you catch it but only lay it in; way off, or no press, it comes off your hands. The window is tighter with him at the spot. In a one-on-one he runs to the spot once it's off the glass and can go up and swat it away or pick it off. He throws oops to himself too (the athletic ones, now and then on a drive), with his timing set by his rating, and you can break his up: △ near the ball as it comes down. Shooting around, cut to the rim after you pass and the man you passed to lobs it up for you (the same timed catch). Every dunk now finishes from the side you came in on instead of always straight in front of the rim. A dunk shakes the camera a little. A step-back keeps you facing the basket. Hold L2 (right-click) with the ball and you stay square to the basket whichever way you move: backing up is a low backward shuffle, sideways a side-step (slower, no sprint); without it, going away from the basket you turn and go (on a phone the back-pedal is automatic). On defense L2 keeps your back to the basket, square to the play, and retreating toward the rim is the same shuffle. He back-pedals the same way: backing it out past the arc facing the basket, retreating on D facing you. And he faces up like your L2: with you within 3.5 m (or backing it out) he stays square to the basket whichever way he moves (backing up at 65%, sideways at 80%); driving, or walking to a spot with nobody near, he turns and goes. Shoot a jumper drifting away from the basket (or with the stick pulled back when you're not driving, or off a step-back) and it's a fadeaway: you drift back and lean through the jump; the defender's contest counts a level less, the green window is 10% smaller. His step-backs push off and hop back smoothly, and he fades away (drifting back and leaning through the jump) off a step-back or sometimes when you're right on him: a little harder to green, but your contest counts for less. After a shot he finishes his jump before he moves. Your jumper rises to the top of the jump right at the perfect release (a quick jump, arms up fast) (hold it and you're coming down). Shot form (both of you): a jumper gathers the ball up from the dribble into both hands at the chest with a dip at the knees, rises to a set point above the forehead (elbow under the ball, wrist cocked, the guide hand on its side), extends to the release at the top and snaps the wrist through (a held follow-through while the guide hand drops), then lands soft on bent knees. A layup reaches up one-handed off the ball's hand, the knee on that side driving up, the off arm out protecting it; a floater is an earlier, softer one-hand push. The ball sits in the hands the whole way. You shoot jumpers right-handed; he's sometimes a lefty. In the air after a shot the stick can't turn you, and after a fadeaway you land and back-pedal a step, still facing the basket, before you can turn and go (he does the same). The shot meter stands upright beside your player, and your energy bar follows under your feet. On the controller and on a phone the camera aims from you at the basket (a spot just behind the rim), so it's in view from the corners too (on a phone you can still drag to look around; it swings back to the basket about a second after you let go). Turned sideways, a ballhandler switches it to the outside hand, away from the defender (or from the basket side when nobody's on him); he does too. Ball handling: every player has a handle rating (55–99). It sets how far the dribble moves carry (a 60 goes about 70% as far as a 90) and how well they work (beating a man, not getting stripped). His comes from his overall and style (slashers higher, shooters lower) and shows when the game starts; yours starts at 72, grows as your moves beat people in games (more for an ankle breaker), is saved, and shows on the shootaround board. Crossovers: a sideways flick of the right stick is a quick crossover right in front of you (a quick shuffle to that side, ~0.9 m at a 90 handle, chainable back to back); with R2 held it's the big burst crossover. Flicking toward the hand the ball's already in is a hesitation (a hard step and a dip on the spot; with R2 a hop out to the ball's side, ~1.2 m at a 90 handle, staying square). Any other move can cut a hesitation short (not another hesitation). Combos: flick during a move and the next one fires the moment it ends (a quick crossover can be cut a little past halfway), so left-right is a double crossover; He strings double crossovers together too. The spin is a full 360° turn toward the new hand's side, the ball tucked at the hip and carried round, the free elbow out, coming out squared up. Every joint, the height and the facing ease into each new pose (~0.1 s), so moves, stances and shots blend instead of snapping, for both of you. After a crossover you square back up to the basket unless the left stick is pointing somewhere. He plays by the same rules: quick crossovers he strings together, big burst ones, side hesitations, squaring up after. The dribbling stance (both of you): knees bent and low standing still, the off arm up in front guarding the ball. Crossovers (and between the legs, behind the back) don't turn you: you shuffle out sideways to the new hand's side, lead leg reaching, leaning into it. A hesitation is a hard step out to the ball's side, the lead leg wide and bent, the weight over it. On defense (both of you): a wide base, knees bent deep, one hand up to bother the shot and one low and out to poke at the ball; sliding sideways the feet push apart and come together without crossing, leaning into the slide; backing up is the backward shuffle. He always slides facing you; you slide while holding L2 (without it you run to recover, in your stance when you stop). Between the legs: hold L2 and flick the right stick sideways (keyboard H, or right-click with J / L). Turned sideways with the ball on the trouble's side (the defender close by, otherwise the basket), a ballhandler switches hands on his own: if the trouble is still out in front (he turned toward it) it goes behind the back; if it's behind him (he turned away) a crossover in front or between the legs. Steals and blocks knock the ball loose instead of handing it over: a poke rolls it a couple of meters, mostly toward the hand that poked it (whoever gets there first has it, and the one who lost it can't grab it straight back; the defender's side has to take it back past the arc); a block swats it away from the blocker's hand. Chase-down blocks: beaten on a drive, run him down from behind and press △ as he goes up for a layup or dunk (a longer reach than a normal block, rarely a foul) and you pin it on the glass; he chases you down the same way when you beat him. The layup's hand follows the left stick: holding it to the right of the rim it's a right-handed layup, to the left a left-handed one, straight at it the hand the ball's in (his follows the side he's heading to). Layups go off the glass (up to the square on your side, then down into the rim) unless you're coming straight down the middle at the basket (within about 20°), where they go straight in; his too. Layups start from 5 m and dunks from 3.8 m; the steps keep your running speed (close in you just go up nearer the rim), and a layup carries on toward the rim in the air. The ball is always in a hand: whichever one has it dribbles it, and the moves that switch hands (crossover, behind the back, spin) take it to the side you go. Every shot is taken squared up to the basket: a small turn happens on the way up, and facing away (more than ~70°) you pivot on your foot first, which the meter waits for (a turnaround's green window is a bit smaller). Chain moves or size him up first and a move that beats him can break his ankles (he can do it to you). Win: the pot and +8 cred (+12 against 82+). To quit, pause (Options / P / the pause button): Quit the game (△ / F, or tap it; on a phone there's also a QUIT button at the top during a game: tap it, then tap SURE? within 2.5 s) calls it off with your money back if nobody's scored yet, otherwise you forfeit the bet (it stops shooting around, and quits a football drive too: money back before the first snap, a forfeit after). Walking off, getting in a car or getting the police after you forfeits; if he gets scared off it's called off and your money comes back. (The 2K research behind this: the search snippets of NBA 2K25/2K26 official notes and guides; their pages couldn't be fetched, so the numbers are adapted, e.g. the green window is 80 ms here vs. about 40–60 ms in 2K, and 40% wider on a phone.)
- One-on-one scoreboard (src/scoreboard.js): top and center, the court's name above a board with YOU and his name, the two scores (02 — 00), a ● POSSESSION under whoever has the ball, and FIRST TO 11 (plus the bet; GAME POINT flashes at one away). A bucket flips that side's number over to the new one, lights the side up gold, floats a +1 / +2 off it and pulses the board. On a phone it's smaller and sits just right of the pause and QUIT buttons. The minimap is hidden during a one-on-one and 33.
- Heat check and on fire (src/heatfx.js), you and him the same: two makes in a row and the ball starts smoking; the next shot is a HEAT CHECK (it says so as he goes up with it). Hit it and you're ON FIRE for up to your next 3 shots while they keep going in: an NBA Jam fire trail on the ball, a 20% bigger green window and unlimited range (the window and the odds stop shrinking past 8 m, and a green from anywhere goes in), and a small explosion at the rim every time it goes through (the heat check make too). In a game, a miss doesn't end it (a miss on fire uses up one of the 3 fire shots): it's over when the ball changes hands without you scoring (he rebounds or steals it; make it, take it, so a bucket keeps it). Shooting around, a missed jumper ends it. The scoreboard tags the name: HEAT CHECK, then the side goes up in flames. In a one-on-one every shot counts; shooting around only jumpers count (layups and dunks don't build or break it); never in 33.
- Dunks (src/dunkpose.js), the whole body for you and him: the gather with the ball at the chest, the free knee driving up off a one-foot takeoff (both knees tucking on a two-hand), leaning in; the arms by style (two-hand: back behind the head, then slammed down; one-hand: up on one hand and snapped through the rim; windmill: the ball hand swings down, back, over the top and through; tomahawk: wound way back with the elbow folded, then whipped forward); hanging on the rim with the legs swinging; then the arms come down and the knees soak up the landing. The ball rides in the hands the whole way.
- Quitting without pausing, in every game (pickup games, shooting around, the 3-point contest, a dice game; any new game adds itself to the same list in main.js `activeGame()`): on the controller or keyboard, hold the touchpad / M for a second (a bar fills); a small "Hold M to quit" / "Hold touchpad to quit" hint sits at the bottom of the screen while you play. On a phone, the QUIT button. The scoreboards and the game bar hide under the pause menu.
- Starting a one-on-one, everyone else at that basket is already off on the sideline. During a check-up you both stand squared up to each other.
- Street dice (src/dice.js): little crowds crouched in a circle on the corners, money down, rolling against the curb — mostly in gang turf (wearing that gang's colors), more at night, now and then on a neutral corner; you can spot them from down the street, dice flying and people jumping up when somebody hits. Walk up and press E / D-pad ↑ / USE to get in (the menu sets your stake, $10–$500); in your own turf, standing with your crew, the same button starts one with them (cee-lo or craps). Crouched in the circle: E / ✕ / ROLL rolls when it's your turn, Q / ○ / LEAVE gets up between rounds; a panel up top shows who's rolled what, the pot and what you're up or down. Cee-lo (three dice): everybody antes the stake, everybody rolls till they hit something: 4-5-6 wins it on the spot, 1-2-3 loses, trips beat a point (a pair and the odd die), the best roll takes the pot (ties split it; nobody hits, antes back). Street craps (two dice): the shooter wins on 7 or 11 coming out, craps out on 2, 3 or 12, anything else is his point and he has to hit it again before a 7 (a seven out passes the dice); when somebody else shoots you bet with him or fade him (◀ ▶ / D-pad / ← →, or sit out), and when you shoot you're on yourself and somebody fades you. The risk is in a rival gang's hood: their dice can be working for them, and winning gets them hot — they might call you a cheat (stand on it with E / ✕, or give it back with Q / ○; standing on it can make them back down, or it goes down: the circle gets up strapped and their people nearby come for you), and walking off up big can get a couple of them following you. Anywhere, somebody might yell "twelve!": the cops are rolling up, everybody scatters, the money on the ground's gone, and it can put a star on you (illegal gambling).
- 3-Point Contest (src/contest.js, src/leaderboard.js): ask a group at a court to play and pick 3-Point Contest · solo from the menu (Money rack ‹ › picks which rack is all money balls). The league's format: five racks round the arc (the corners, the wings, the top), five balls each, four worth 1 and the last (the money ball, blue) worth 2; the money rack's five are all worth 2; between the wings and the top, two deep balls (gold) a couple of meters behind the arc, worth 3 each. 70 seconds, 40 is perfect. 3, 2, 1, go: you grab each ball off the rack (it runs front to back along your left, pointing at the basket; a quick turn and reach, and the rest roll forward to the front slot) and shoot it with the shot meter (the timing badge as usual; the green window is 20% smaller than in a game); you run to the next station on your own; a ball let go of before the horn counts if it goes in. The board up top has the clock, your score, your best and a dot per ball (green made, red missed). After the horn: your score, a new best, and the leaderboard. In the published game the leaderboard is shared by everyone who plays it (the top 10, with your rank); anywhere it can't reach that it shows your best runs on this device. Run it back (✕ / Enter) or Done (○ / Esc). For now it's solo; 2–5 player contests (3 players: 2 rounds; 5 players: 3 rounds) wait for online multiplayer (ROADMAP).
- Jump shots go up quicker: every jumper (yours and his, in every game) is 28% faster from the gather to the release than it was, and its green window is 28% smaller to match (a three from the arc is about 66 ms). Layups, floaters and dunks are unchanged.
- Shot timing badge: your shot's timing shows under your energy bar, the same spot every time: ◇ EXCELLENT / PERFECT RELEASE in mint green (it pops in from 95%, the edge flashes once, a green pulse rings out on the floor at your feet), SLIGHTLY EARLY / LATE in warm amber, EARLY / LATE / VERY EARLY / LATE in muted coral; the second line is how open you were (in a game) or how many ms off (shooting around). It holds about 0.8 s and fades.
- Defense: the stance has both hands low and out; hold the right stick (I on the keyboard, HANDS on a phone) to keep your hands up and contest a shot without jumping (it counts toward the contest, the same as a jump; he puts his hands up on your shot when he's on you). Moving in the stance steps instead of gliding: sliding sideways the lead foot lifts and reaches out and the trail foot closes in, with a bob in the hips, the steps going by how far you actually move; closing out or backing up it's short choppy steps. The two of you can't walk through each other (pushed apart).

Afterwards he walks back and they drive off (or it's the arrest). Traffic-ticket stops (when tickets are on) play out the same way
- Pursuit tactics (src/pursuit.js): while they can see you driving, cruisers ahead race to cut you off where you'll be in a couple of seconds; at 2+ stars the closest one behind lines up on your rear quarter and PITs you (you spin out; one try every ~8 s, between about 15 and 65 mph); slow to a crawl and they box you in (one across your nose, one alongside) for the arrest. Police radio chatter under the wanted stars (where you're headed, lost visual, PIT, air support)
- Lying low: park dead still for a few seconds among parked cars (a lot or the curb) or under a freeway deck while nobody's watching, or crouch among cars on foot: cruisers only spot you from about 14 m (officers on foot 9 m), and under a deck the helicopter can't see you. Move and it's over. A meter under the stars shows how close you are to losing them (LOSING THEM / LYING LOW); the search circle widens slowly the longer they look
- Gunfire is heard (main.js gunshot): shots fired — by you or anyone, hit or not — put nearby gang members on alert (gun up, facing the sound, the jumpy ones crouched) and rivals who can see you were the shooter come for you; officers on foot draw and turn toward it; a cop who can see where the shots came from (up to 70 m) is a witness; and patrol cars that hear it (up to ~170 m) drive over with their lights on to look around — find you there soon after and you're wanted (police radio: "shots fired near..."). Gang wars are left to the gangs
- Police search: they comb the area where they last saw you (behind cover, around corners), hear gunshots and running footsteps, and keep tracking you if they watched you duck. Escape by leaving the search circle unseen or staying hidden long enough
- 4+ stars: more cruisers, SWAT vans with four-man teams in body armor, and a police helicopter: air support gets called in when you hit 4 stars and takes off from the precinct roof 20–30 s later. It tracks you with a searchlight (buildings block its view) and can be shot down; at 5 stars its door gunner opens fire
- Roadblocks at 4+ stars: police cars drive ahead of you and park across the road (spike strips at 5 stars)
- Civilians react to gunfire: some dive flat, some duck in place, some run to cover and hide, the rest run
- NPC traffic that follows lanes and stops for cars and people
- NPC pedestrians who stay on the sidewalks and cross only at the zebra crosswalks by each intersection (even when running from violence or walking off after a deal), and flee from violence. The exceptions are people diving for cover, people getting out of a car, and a buyer you wave over

Hovering a marker on the full map (mouse, or the controller's cursor) — or a row in the legend — makes it grow and shows a label saying what it is. The map's legend (open/close with L / △ or the tab in the corner; remembered) lists everything on it (you, the waypoint, each gang's territory, unclaimed blocks, turf wars, hangouts, police, hospital, chop shop, gun shop, garage, safehouse, stash, plugs, stores, claim spots, police units, the helicopter); the helicopter shows on the radar and the map as a little helicopter pointing where it's flying, with its rotor spinning, lights flashing and its searchlight spot on the ground (brighter when it has you); D-pad ↑↓ / arrow keys step through it (or click a row) and the cursor jumps to that place

Walking and running the elbows bend with the pace; a gun arm locks straight down the sights with the other arm bent in to support it; a punch cocks the elbow and snaps it out; guns and badges are held in the hand. In basketball the elbows and wrists drive the dribble, the set point and the follow-through.

After a couple of quiet minutes they walk back to the car and drive off. Homies: free every 3 minutes, 2 of them (3 once your base is tier 2–3, 4 at tier 4), carrying whatever crew guns you've bought. Heavy squad: 4 shooters with rifles and vests in a van, $1,500 (bank, then cash), every 5 minutes, from Hustler street cred. A "Call a player" row is there for when multiplayer lets your crewmates answer.

Tier 1 trap house (a flag, boarded windows), 2 apartment spot / duplex (+ a banner with the gang's name), 3 warehouse / nightclub (+ neon), 4 penthouse / mansion (+ a flag on the roof). Bigger bases have more guards (4 / 7 / 10 / 13) and cars. What a block can be depends on its district (the Projects and Midtown top out at 2, the Docks and the Strip at 3, Downtown, the Financial District and the Heights at 4); a gang moves to the best site on its own turf once its strength has held for a couple of minutes, only while you're well away, and its kingpin and home crew move with it (City News reports it). Rival bases bank money every game day (their blocks and protection rackets): hold E / ✕ at the door to rob it — the gang comes for you, and their beef with you jumps. Taking the base block in a turf war still wipes the gang off the map. Your crew's base upgrades in place at your hangout. The map shows each base with its tier (legend: strength, tier, stash)
- Gang AI with "heart": low-heart fighters run, mid-heart fighters take cover (building corners, crates, parked cars) and pop out to shoot, reckless ones charge in the open; nerve drops when friends go down, and police in the fight make everyone likelier to run

Walk up and press E to grab it. Two payouts sitting there brings a warning; the third gets the stash hit by the biggest rival gang (they take half)

Won't serve you with stars. You start with fists and a pistol (`CFG.START_ALL_GUNS` gives everything for testing); what you buy is saved
- Body armor: a blue bar next to health that soaks up 70% of each hit until it's worn through
- Mod garage, Lou's Mod Garage (M on the radar): stop the car in the ring and press E. Repair, engine / brakes / armor in three levels each, and respray (with stars: drops one; at 1 star the cops lose you). Mods stay on that car
- Drug dealing (src/drugs.js): three plugs (D on the radar) — Smokey (weed), Doc (pills), Marco (coke). Open the phone and press R / □ for Contacts to set up a pickup: a small pickup, a re-up, or, at Kingpin, a gang run (big load; `CFG.GANG_RUNS_ANY_RANK` unlocks it for testing). Drive to the plug, get out and walk up to them (a ring marks them), press E / D-pad ↑ and wait a few seconds for the handoff, then get it back to the hangout with no cops on you. Risk grows with the product and the load: the cops can raid the spot during the handoff (2–3 stars): a short cinematic — letterbox bars, a flashing RAID title, the camera swinging (at most half a lap, about 4.5 s) and rising into a high, wide-angle shot over the street — as 3–4 cruisers come screaming in from both ends of the road, in both lanes, each pulling up at its own spot 10–15 m from you (clear of your car and anything else in the way) before officers pile out, or the nearest rival gang sends real crews in their cars to chase you down on the way back (gang runs can get both). The plug fronts it, and you pay in person: walk up to the plug with the cash on you and press E / ✕ (Contacts → "Pay back (in person)" sets a waypoint). Three load sizes: a small pickup is the safest but the plug charges the most per unit (weed ~44% margin, pills/coke ~46%); a re-up is bigger, cheaper per unit (~60%) and riskier; a gang run (Kingpin) is the biggest and cheapest (~70%) and riskiest. You have 30 minutes. Late, the plug gives one 10-minute extension with interest: +20% if it wasn't your fault (raid, bust, robbed, killed, market crashed in your district), +50% if it was (you had the money on you or in the stash, or sat on the product for 10+ minutes without selling). Miss that too and there's a hit on you (+50%, shooters in a black car from the plug's corner every 2.5 minutes until you pay). After you die or get busted, the shooters fall back for 5 minutes. Short on cash: Contacts → "Ask for work" gives a job (deliver a package across town in 4 minutes, or collect from someone who runs when he sees you; knock him down) that takes ~40% off the debt, and the shooters hold off while you're on it. Trust per plug: paying on time +8; late −12 (bad luck) or −25 (your fault); a hit −20; a failed job −5. Low trust means higher prices; below 20 the plug wants cash up front at the pickup; below 10 there's no extension. Pushing it: in the last quarter of the clock (or once you're late), Contacts → "Push it" sells cheap (10% off, sliding to 35% off) and buyers come faster and buy more — but never below what you paid per unit. Gang runs split 40% to you, 60% to your homies, who sell it off into the turf stash over the next three payouts. Busted: the cops take everything you're holding
- Drug market (src/market.js): supply and demand per district (Downtown, Financial District, Government District, The Heights, Midtown, The Projects, The Strip, The Docks) and per drug. Each district has its own taste (the Financial District and the Heights pay for coke, the Projects smoke weed, the Strip likes pills). Price = base × demand ÷ supply (0.5×–2.2×). Selling a lot in one place taps buyers out; random events shift demand (party weekend, clinic shut down, dispensary opens). Supply is citywide per drug (the plugs) plus local (rival gangs dealing on their turf): raids cut a drug's supply and make plugs charge more; plugs can be killed (that drug dries up until a new plug takes over, inheriting your tab); each rival gang has a kingpin (♛) at its home base, and killing one dries up their corners; your big runs flood the area and push prices down. Everything drifts back to normal over several minutes. `CFG.MARKET_HOT` (on while testing) makes the market hot: demand 1.5× everywhere, demand events every ~45 s, buyers 3× as often A drive-up buyer stuck in traffic nearby gets out and walks over. Buyers come more often where demand for what you hold is high and want what's popular there. Phone → Market app shows every district's price for each drug with ▲▼ trends and why (✕ sets a waypoint); on the full map, H / □ cycles a price heat map (blue cheap → red high) with district prices
- President's approval rating (src/president.js, pause menu → President's orders): driven by a crime level that every crime raises (weighted: a cop killed counts far more than a carjacking; NPC turf wars count by their body count; drug killings count) and that fades over ~10 minutes. Approval drifts toward 65% in a quiet city and falls as crime climbs; arrests, drug raids and street sweeps help, suspects escaping hurts. Crackdowns and the war on drugs read as strong when crime is high and heavy-handed when it's quiet. Big swings make City News; the panel shows the rating, the crime level and what's driving it
- War on drugs (pause menu → President's orders): Hands off / Tough on drugs / All-out war. Each step scares off buyers (demand −15%), seizes supply (−20%, so prices and plug costs climb), makes raids on pickups likelier and puts more undercover cops among buyers; all-out war adds street sweeps that clear a district's corners every few minutes
- Selling on rival turf (src/turfrisk.js): their buyers are loyal (pay 25% less, come less often) unless that gang's kingpin is dead (then their buyers come to you and pay 25% more). Each sale can get you noticed — more near their people, on their home block, and the more you sell on one block: first a warning text, then (within 3 minutes) their crew opens fire or drives over to find you. Sometimes one of their soldiers walks up for a 30% cut of what you made there: pay (E / ✕) and you can sell there for 5 minutes, refuse (Q / ○) and they come at you — drop and they take everything you're holding. Selling there builds beef with that gang (cools off over time): a warning text at 50, and at 100 they hit your stash (or one of your blocks if it's empty)
- After a deal buyers head back the way they came and out of sight; one who parked and walked over gets back in their car and drives off. Undercover cops don't walk away: badge out, gun on you, a short "POLICE" cinematic, and backup cruisers that were already waiting nearby race in from both ends of the street within a couple of seconds, jump out and aim from behind their doors. They hold fire: stand still for a few seconds and you're arrested (fine, product seized); run off or pull a gun and they open fire. Raids work the same way. What you carry is at risk: killed by opps (a rival gang), they take all your cash and product; killed by the plug's shooters, they take all your cash (it comes off what you owe) and the product; killed by the cops, they seize the product; any other death (a crash, a fire) drops the product where you went down (gone after 90 s). Busted: a fine and all your product. Dirty cops: a patrol stop at 1–2 stars (not a raid or a sting) can be a dirty cop — 20% of the time, more at night (10 PM–4 AM), most in Southgate and Harbor. Each stop shows 1–2 details about the officer; dirty ones slip (came alone, no siren, unmarked car, "what you got on you?", waves you around the corner, hasn't cuffed you, or a name you've seen in City News misconduct stories) but not always, and straight cops sometimes come alone. E / ✕ go quietly: a straight cop arrests you; a dirty cop takes everything (cash and product) and lets you walk, wanted level cleared. Q / ○ offer a bribe: a dirty cop takes your product and half your cash; a straight cop adds a bribery charge (bigger fine, −25 cred). `CFG.TEST_DIRTY_COP` forces 'dirty' or 'clean'
- Selling: while you're holding and not on a run or wanted, buyers walk up (or drive up and pull over at the curb). A buyer across the street walks to the curb on their side and waves you down: E / ✕ waves them over and they cut straight across; Q / ○ or ignoring them for 12 s sends them on their way. Buyers on foot come to whichever car window is closer, usually the curb side. E / ✕ sells, Q / ○ turns them down. Some are undercover cops (2 stars; sometimes you get a tell, like "shoes are too clean"); some snatch it and run (knock them down to get it back). Higher street cred brings buyers more often (every 90 s as a Nobody down to 25 s as Kingpin), bigger buys, better prices and fewer robberies
- Safehouse (S on the radar): $5,000. You respawn there after being wasted or busted, start there, and can rest to heal and save
- Cars take damage, catch fire and explode; ammo drops from downed enemies

- Dialogue voice: homies, plugs and street buyers talk in African American Vernacular English, written from research (kept outside the repo) — grammar over respelling, light slang, coded product talk, short calm threats; each plug has their own voice (Smokey laid back, Doc clipped and cold, Marco an older OG), homies text warm, buyers sound anxious, and undercover cops sometimes give themselves away by naming the drug like a cop would. Lines live in `VOICES` / `ASKS` in src/drugs.js and the homie texts in src/shops.js. No slurs and no real-gang vocabulary

## Playing on a phone

Open the game link on your phone and turn it sideways. Touch controls switch on at the first touch (src/touch.js); they work like a virtual PS5 controller, so every prompt, menu and the phone work the same way.
- Left thumb: a joystick that appears wherever you touch the left side. On foot it moves you (push it all the way out to sprint); in a car it steers, and up/down is gas/brake. Driving is tuned for thumbs: steering is gentle near the center (full lock at the edge of a wider sideways throw), turns less at speed and moves the wheel smoothly; push up past a third for gas and it stays on while you steer until you pull back below the middle (same for the brake); the camera swings back behind the car sooner.
- Right thumb: drag anywhere on the right side to look around. Drag off FIRE to aim while shooting. AIM is a toggle (tap on, tap off) and gets the controller's aim assist.
- On foot: FIRE, AIM, ○ (punch), ✕ / ○ as yes / no only while someone's asking (a buyer's offer, a corner tax, a ticket; in a car too: the rest of the time they're hidden), □ reload, △ get in a car, DUCK, GUN (tap to swap weapons, hold for the inventory strip, tap again to close it) and USE, which lights up yellow when there's something to do.
- Driving: GAS, BRAKE, HAND BRAKE, △ exit, ✕ / ○ for prompts (tickets, deals), USE.
- Top row: pause, 📱 phone, MAP, CAR (test car while that testing switch is on).
- Phone, shop menus and the inventory strip: a D-pad plus ✕ / ○ on the left. Rows can be tapped too: the first tap selects (so you can read the details), the second buys or opens.
- Map: drag to pan (when zoomed in), pinch to zoom, tap to drop a waypoint; buttons for zoom, legend, heat map and close.
- Phones get "Phone" graphics automatically (src/quality.js): lower render resolution, no antialiasing, cheaper and shorter shadows, a shorter view distance (fog closes in sooner), 60% of the people, 70% of the traffic and fewer parked cars drawn far away. Switch between Phone and Full in the pause menu (saved), or add `?quality=phone` / `?quality=full` to the address. `?touch` forces the touch controls on.
- The HUD, phone, shops, map, pause menu and title screen are laid out for small landscape screens; in portrait you're asked to turn the phone. Android goes full screen when you tap PLAY (on iPhone, use Share → Add to Home Screen for full screen).

## Street layout clean-up
Each district lays out its own street grid, so worldplan.js tidies up where they meet (`tidyStreets`): a street that mostly runs right alongside a bigger one is dropped (no overlapping pavement), and a street ending a few meters from an existing junction is moved onto it (one crossing instead of a jog). Stop signs and signal poles stand on the curb clear of every road, following the street round its bends; where there's no clear curb (two junctions very close, at the interchanges) the pole is left out.

## Merging and the camera under the freeway
- Entrances: every acceleration lane runs on alongside the freeway (round the ring's corners too) for up to 150 m, then tapers into the right lane over up to 70 m; it ends before the next exit. Where the next exit is close (the ring's interchanges are ~400 m apart), it's an auxiliary lane that runs straight on into that exit: merge left anywhere along it, or stay in it to get off. AI cars can move over anywhere along these lanes too (fwyplan.js `addTails`, highway.js).
- Under the freeway, the camera stays below the deck (it eases down just before you drive under and back up after), so the deck never blocks the view (main.js `updateCamera`, highway.js `ceilingAbove`).

## Interchange entrances
- The ramps are laid out for the real cross streets (all avenues, 20 m curb to curb): exits come down square to the curb, the loop entrances peel off the curb lane, the diagonal entrances leave from the curb. The short links between the middle of the street and each ramp end are for traffic only (inside the street, nothing drawn), so there are no paved blobs at the ramp ends.
- Outside the ring the ground is levelled over the interchange land and eases back into the hills around it, so the ramps and the street meet at the same height.
- Roads on the hills: the ground under a road takes its height from the nearest piece of that road (it used to take the first piece within reach, which buried downhill stretches under the grass).
- Entrance signs are small green signs on a pole by the curb ("RING HWY EAST ↗"), on the ground there, moved clear of roads and ramps. Street lights and stop/signal poles are never placed in a road, on a ramp, or in the ramp land.
- The diagonal entrances peel off the curb lane in a smooth right-hand curve (no square turn), well clear of the exit opposite. Traffic only turns onto an entrance from the side where it's a right turn (`approach` on the ramp-end node; traffic.js `pickNext`). Ramp ends ride a little above the street and grass where they meet, and the camera's near plane is 0.3 m (sharper depth far off), so the grass doesn't flicker through.

## Interchange editor (testing tool)
Pause → "Interchange editor" (switch: `RAMP_EDITOR` in src/config.js). A top-down view of one side of one interchange (◀ ▶ to move between them) with its three ramps: exit (red), loop entrance (green), diagonal entrance (blue). Each ramp is a smooth curve through control points: drag a dot to move it, tap a ramp to add a dot, select a dot and DELETE to remove it, UNDO, RESET. The square dot (where the ramp joins the freeway) stays put. Drag empty space to pan; pinch, the + / − buttons or the mouse wheel zoom.
- Curves: tap a dot to select it and its two curve handles appear (yellow diamonds): drag one to bend the curve there (like a drawing program's Bezier handles); SMOOTH puts that dot back to automatic. The ramps are cubic Bezier pieces between the dots.
- SNAP (on by default): drag a ramp's street end (the exit's last dot, an entrance's first) near a street and it locks into that street's curb lane, the ramp's outer edge in line with the curb and its direction along the street, so it splits off (or merges in) seamlessly; a yellow ring marks a snapped end. Traffic takes a snapped entrance from the direction it leaves the street.
- "EDITING: ALL" changes the design every interchange on that freeway shares (ring or crosstown); "THIS ONE ONLY" changes just that side (and wins over the shared design).
- SAVE & DRIVE stores the edits in the browser (`crimecity.rampedits.v1`) and reloads; the city is rebuilt from them (ramp heights follow the old profile along the new curve; the land kept clear for ramps, the street links and the entrance signs follow the ramps) and you start on the cross street facing that interchange.
- COPY shows the edits as text to send over, so they can be built in for good (fwyplan.js `EDITS` / `editableRamp`, rampeditor.js).
- The ring interchanges now use the design made in the editor (fwyplan.js `BAKED`): the loop and diagonal entrances peel off the avenue's curb lane, and the exit curves down and merges into a road alongside the freeway (on the far edge of the interchange land) that runs on to the avenue. Out of town that road is just the stretch from the exit to the avenue. The editor starts from this design; RESET goes back to it.

## Signs, signals, lights and markings
- Traffic lights stand on the far side of the junction (on the right, just past the crosswalk), where a driver waiting at the line can see them; two approaches wanting the same corner share a pole. Stop signs stand on the near right-hand curb. Poles are never in a road or on a ramp (more room on the hill roads, which are strips laid on the terrain).
- Street name signs: green blades on the corner pole of each junction (the stop sign's or the light's), one per named street, each running along the street it names (all names in one texture: one draw call).
- Street lights keep 2 m from sign and signal poles; hydrants, trash cans and benches keep clear of all of them and never stand in a road.
- Markings (center dashes, parking lines, stop lines, crosswalks) sit just above the road surface everywhere, including the hill roads (city.js `roadY`); the center line runs on round bends (it only stops short at real junctions). Hill roads have a wider flat strip either side so the hillside doesn't poke through their edges.
