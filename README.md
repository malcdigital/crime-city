# Crime City

A GTA-style online city where every player lives a role: president, police, bounty hunter, gang member, business owner or entertainer. See the game design document for the full vision.

This repo holds **prototype 0.1**: a single-player 3D browser build of the core gang member vs. police loop.

## Play it locally

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Then open the address Vite prints (usually http://localhost:5173). Desktop browser with keyboard and mouse.

## What's in 0.1

- A 14×14-block city (about 1.1 km across) in eight districts, each with its own look (src/districts.js holds the layout, special places and starting turf):
  - The Heights (NW): big houses with yards and trees
  - Financial District (N): glass towers and a plaza
  - Government District (NE): stone civic buildings with columns, City Hall (domed) and the police station
  - Midtown (W, and south of Downtown): mixed mid-rise, the hospital, your crew's park, the gun shop and safehouse
  - Downtown (center): tall city blocks
  - The Strip (E): dark low clubs with neon
  - The Projects (SW): long low brick buildings, the Southside Kings' corner
  - The Docks (SE): warehouses, stacked shipping containers, the chop shop
  Districts show as slightly different shades on the radar and map, with faint names on the full map. Traffic and pedestrians fill the streets around you (cars more than 380 m away and people more than 300 m away are replaced nearer). Static buildings are merged per 2×2-block chunk to keep draw calls down, and collision checks use a spatial grid
- Day and night (src/daynight.js): a full day is 48 real minutes (one real second is half a game minute); sessions start at 9:00 PM. The sky, fog, sun (moonlight at night) and ambient light follow the clock, with a warm glow at dawn and dusk; after dark building windows light up and the street lights come on (soft pools of light on the road)
- Streets (src/streets.js): traffic lights at the busy intersections (Downtown, the Financial and Government districts, the Strip, every third street in Midtown — 102 of them), all-way stop signs everywhere else (123), white stop lines on every approach, and street lights along every sidewalk (two per block side). Signals run north-south then east-west (green 10 s, yellow 2.5 s, all-red 1 s), staggered street to street. Traffic stops at the line on red, goes through a yellow only when it's too close to stop, and at stop signs comes to a full stop and goes in the order cars arrived once the intersection is clear. Pedestrians wait on the curb for the walk phase (they only start across with 6+ seconds of green left, and hurry across); cars stop for anyone in front of them. Police, gang cars and backup ignore the rules. Poles and posts are solid
- Clock: top right of the HUD, e.g. "Tue 9:42 PM · Day 3" (the day count is saved; day 1 is a Monday)
- Taxis and buses (src/travel.js): fast travel with a quick cut scene (you get in, it pulls away, a title card with where you're going, the time and the fare, then you're dropped at the curb). The clock moves on by however long the drive would take. Nobody gives you a ride while you're wanted.
  - Taxi: walk up to a taxi on the street and press USE / E to hail it; it pulls over while you pick your map waypoint or a place (home, the hangout, the gun shop, the garage, the nearest bank, the chop shop, the hospital, any district). $20 plus $1 per block (bank first, then cash).
  - Bus: at any of the 23 bus stops, press USE / E and pick any stop on either route ($5, no waiting). The ★ stop is the one closest to your waypoint.
- Highways (src/highway.js): an elevated ring around the city (over the edge roads) and a crosstown highway over 7th Street, 8 m up, with ten ramps (two on each side of the ring, two onto the crosstown; each takes over one block of an avenue, with a green sign at the bottom). Speed limit 65 (the speedometer shows it), no lights or stop signs, guard rails, pillars down the middle of the streets below. Traffic, police chases and the GPS use them. They show in amber on the radar and the map. Police don't get out of their cars up there, and nobody walks up except you
: headlights and taillights come on after dark; each headlight throws its own soft beam straight ahead on the road (about as wide as the lamp where it starts, spreading to about 4 m and fading out about 10 m on); brake lights show whenever a driver brakes or holds the car still; delivery trucks blink their hazards when double-parked. A police car with its lights going washes the street around it red and blue (strongest at night). Empty cars stay dark
- Daily rhythms (src/rhythm.js): how many cars and people are out depends on the hour and the district. Morning rush (7–9 AM) and evening rush (4:30–6:30 PM) are the busiest times; the Financial and Government districts fill up in working hours and empty out at night; the Strip comes alive after dark (packed 10 PM–2 AM, about 1.8× the daytime crowd); the Heights stay quiet; around 4 AM the whole city is close to empty (about 10 people and 10 cars around you). The vehicle mix changes too: taxis Downtown and on the Strip, box trucks at the Docks and in working hours, sports cars in the Heights and on the Strip at night
- Vehicles on the road: sedans, sports cars, vans, taxis (yellow, roof sign), box trucks (which sometimes double-park mid-block with their hazards on to make a drop; traffic goes around them) and city buses
- Buses (src/transit.js): two routes loop around the middle of the city with a bus shelter every third street (23 stops: roof, glass back wall, bench, BUS sign at the curb). Buses obey the lights, stop at each shelter for a few seconds, and people wait at the stops, get on and get off. Long vehicles stop at the line by their front bumper
- Parked cars (src/parking.js): about 1,600 cars along the curbs, more in the residential parts of town (the Heights, Midtown, the Projects, the Strip) and fewer in the business districts and at the Docks, none near the corners, bus stops or spawn points. Far away they're cheap stand-ins (three instanced meshes for the whole city); within 28 m each one becomes a real car you can hit, push, shoot or steal (a car alarm goes off about a third of the time: a small Car theft charge). A car that gets moved or wrecked leaves an empty space that fills again later
- Street props (src/props.js): a fire hydrant on every block (hit one with a car and it breaks off and sprays water about 6 m up for half a minute), trash cans near the corners (more in the busy districts), benches mid-block in Midtown, Downtown, the Heights and around the parks. Cars knock them over (they slow you down; hydrants hurt a little); you can't walk through them; they're put back once you're well away. Litter on the sidewalks and in the gutters and graffiti tags on the walls in the poorer parts of town (the Projects most, then the Docks, the Strip and Midtown; none in the Heights or the Financial District)
- Traffic enforcement (src/enforcement.js): police patrol cars cruise with the traffic when nobody's wanted (about 3 around you, 4 Downtown and in the Government and Financial districts and on the Strip, more or fewer with the police budget; plain blue dots on the radar). Run a red light or do more than 55 in the city's 45 zone (the speedometer shows the limit when you're over it) where a patrol can see you and it lights you up. Pull over and you get a ticket ($150 red light, $200 speeding; bank first, then cash; it goes to the city treasury). Holding product? There's a 35% chance the officer smells it and it's an arrest (which can still turn out to be a dirty cop). Don't stop within about 20 seconds, or drive off during the stop, and it's a chase (1 star). After a ticket they leave you alone for a minute. Once you're wanted, patrol cars nearby join the chase
- Money (src/bank.js): cash on you, the bank, and home stashes — you go to each in person.
  - Cash on you is what plugs take (they don't do wires) and what you lose when opps drop you or rob you. The HUD shows your bank balance and stash total under your cash.
  - City Bank (B on the map; branches in the Financial District, Midtown and the Docks): safe from robbers, open 9 AM–6 PM only, 2% to deposit, no limit at the counter, and a $250 monthly fee (an in-game month is 30 game days, about 24 hours of play). Shops (gun shop, garage, homes, safehouse) take bank money first, then cash.
  - ATMs (blue squares, 10 around the city): withdraw only, up to $2,000 per game day (resets at midnight), any time.
  - Homes for sale (⌂ on the map): trap apartment in the Projects, Midtown condo, Strip loft, Heights house, Downtown penthouse (base prices $3,000 / $8,000 / $12,000 / $25,000 / $60,000), plus the safehouse ($5,000).
  - Property market (src/property.js; phone → Property app lists every property, its price, ▲▼ trend and why): value = base × district demand × crime × turf × the city's mood. District demand drifts and moves with news (a new transit line, a factory closing); every crime raises that district's crime level (fades over ~10 minutes) and high crime knocks up to 35% off; a rival gang's turf takes 10% off and a turf war in the district 8%; the President's approval nudges everything. Prices are round numbers (3 significant figures: $3,000, $25,500, $1,020,000) with the 6% sales tax already in them — a $60,000 home costs $60,000, of which $3,396 is sales tax. Selling (at the property): you get the price less the sales tax in it, into the bank (clear the stash first; it comes with you).
  - Owning costs: property tax of 0.25% of the current value every game week (7 game days, 5 h 36 min of play), and upkeep (utilities, maintenance) every game day, progressive on the current value like tax brackets — 0.10% of the first $5,000, 0.20% of the part up to $20,000, 0.35% up to $50,000, 0.50% above (trap apartment ~$3/day, penthouse ~$195/day). Bills come out of the bank, then that property's stash; anything left is owed. Still owing a week later is a warning (a strike); a second strike and the city seizes the property and its stash. Pay what you owe at the property. Taxes (sales and property, plus the 6% tax on services that's already inside every upkeep bill) go to the city treasury, shown in the pause menu under the President's approval. Each home (and the safehouse) has a stash: no fees and open any time, plus rest (heal and save). Stashes can be robbed: rarely at random, and as payback when a gang has beef with you (they hit your biggest stash if it's bigger than the turf stash); robbers take half. You respawn at the nearest home you own.
  - Plug debt: money in the bank only counts as "you had the money" (the +50% late penalty) if the bank was open at some point while the clock ran. Cash on you and in your stashes always counts.
  - NPC Presidents (src/politics.js; shown at the top of President's orders): each has a name, a made-up party and a political leaning that sets the budget, the war on drugs, gang crackdowns and tax rates. Law & Order (right: more police, tough war on drugs, crackdowns on the biggest gang when crime is high), Small Government (libertarian: tiny budget, low taxes, hands off), Moderate (center: balanced), Progressive (left: community programs and public works, drugs as a health issue, higher taxes). Rarely (15%) an extreme: Iron Fist (far right: max police, all-out war on drugs, crackdowns on every gang including yours, a night curfew with even more cops after dark), Free City (far libertarian: police slashed, taxes nearly gone, weed legal — no weed raids or undercover weed buyers, but legal shops push street prices down ~30%), People's Republic (far left: huge programs, 0.8% weekly property tax, buys out private service companies — yours too, at market price — and won't sell them), The Boss (corrupt: skims a quarter of the treasury daily, twice the dirty cops). Elections every game week (7 game days): 50%+ approval and they're re-elected (up to 4 terms in a row; then someone new from the same party takes over); below that someone new comes in, usually from the other side. Taking over any order in the pause menu (budget, war on drugs, a crackdown) puts you in charge until you hand it back; a new President takes the orders back.
  - Economy tuning (src/tuning.js): every money number in one place (budget costs, city debt, tax hikes, company pricing, neighborhood and housing-market dynamics, approval weights). Press ` (backquote) for the economy debug panel: President and approval (with what's moving it), treasury/income/budget, every district's wealth and crime, companies, gang protection income, property prices, taxes and upkeep.
  - City finances: normal spending is about 80% of a quiet city's income. The city can borrow (the treasury goes below zero, 1% interest a day) down to 5 days of income; past that, the budget is only partly paid. Deep in debt (2+ days of income), the President raises taxes 25% at a time (up to +50%; libertarians cut spending instead), and lowers them again once 3 days of income are saved. Debt, shortfalls and tax hikes all cost approval.
  - Approval follows the city: crime above normal, budget shortfalls, debt, tax hikes and how the neighborhoods are doing (plus a honeymoon for a new President), moving over about a game day.
  - Housing market: a citywide index drifts up over time (about +0.1% a game day, roughly +16% over 150 days), with wobble and the odd boom or slump in City News — property can be an investment now.
  - Companies sell for about 180 days of profit. When private owners control half or more of the city's services, City Hall taxes their profits 25% (not under a libertarian President; a People's Republic buys them out instead).
  - City budget (src/budget.js; pause menu → President's orders): the treasury (taxes plus city-run companies' profits, about $3,300 a game day; starts with $10,000) pays for the city every game day. Police (Cut / Normal / Boosted / Max, $0–$5,000 a day): more cops per wanted star (×0.6 to ×1.6), the helicopter up sooner, fewer dirty cops; cuts do the opposite. Public works (None / Normal / Big, $0–$2,500): lifts every neighborhood. Community programs (None / Some / Lots, $0–$2,000): crime fades faster and the poorest neighborhoods get a lift. The war on drugs ($0 / $1,500 / $3,500) and each gang crackdown ($600) cost money too. By default the President decides each day — tougher policing when crime is high, building when it's quiet, spending savings down and then staying within income — and City News reports changes. Click a line to set it yourself (you decide until you hand it back). If the treasury can't cover the day, everything gets part of its money and part of its effect, and the President's approval drops.
  - Where upkeep goes (src/economy.js): every game day everyone with a home pays upkeep — you and the city's residents (about $45 a block, more in richer districts, ~$8,900 a day citywide). It's split between four service companies: Metro Power & Water (40%, Government District), FixRight Maintenance (25%, the Projects), SparkleClean (20%, Midtown) and CityNet (15%, the Strip). Of each company's take: 6% is tax on services (to the treasury); 30% of the rest is profit — to you if you own the company (buy or sell it at its office, ◆ on the map; prices from ~$15,000 to ~$54,000, about 40 days of profit, sales tax included), or to the city if nobody does (city-run services); 10% is protection money for whichever gang holds the office's block (your crew's goes into the turf stash with a text; SparkleClean sits on your turf); the rest is wages and supplies, spent in the neighborhoods.
  - Neighborhood wealth: each district has a level (Rich / Well-off / Comfortable / Getting by / Struggling) from its starting wealth (Financial District richest, the Projects poorest) times how it's doing — spending there lifts it, crime drags it down, over a few game days. Richer districts' drug buyers pay up to ~6% more (the Projects ~8% less), and home values follow the neighborhood. Phone → Property shows the companies and every neighborhood with ▲▼.
  - Tuning in `CFG`: `BANK_OPEN`, `BANK_CLOSE`, `BANK_DEPOSIT_FEE`, `BANK_MONTHLY_FEE`, `ATM_DAILY_LIMIT`
- Third-person on foot (walk, sprint, crouch, punch) and driving (4 car types, handbrake)
- Cover: press C / L3 next to a wall, crate or car to snap to it, crate or car and stay there; slide along it, lean out at corners or pop up over low cover to aim; push away or press again to leave
- Crouching bends at the knees; crouch behind low cover to block bullets and break line of sight
- Two weapon slots, Fortnite-style: you carry at most two guns (fists when a slot is empty). Tap L1 / Tab (or 1 / 2, or the mouse wheel) to swap between them. Buying a third gun replaces the one in your hand. Ammo is kept per gun type. Hold L1 / Tab to show the inventory strip: both weapons (with mag / reserve), all your ammo, cash and armor, and your drugs. The strip's top row is your two weapons, an Ammo tab, cash and a Drugs tab; ✕ / ↑ on a tab opens it (its contents show in a row above) and ↓ / ○ closes it. While the strip is open, D-pad ← → (arrow keys) picks a tile, ✕ (E) gives it, □ (G) drops it: a press moves a set amount (one mag of ammo, $100, one unit of product), holding moves all of it, guns go whole. Dropped things land in front of you and can be picked back up (step away first); a dropped gun with both slots full asks to swap. Giving goes to a homie following you (they use the gun, or sell the product into the stash); cash and ammo will go to other players once there's multiplayer
- Getting out of a moving car: 30 mph or less, you brake hard (about 1 g) to a stop and step out; faster, you dive head-first out the driver's door, take impact damage scaled to speed, tumble along the ground (about 0.95 g of friction; walls hurt) and get back up, while the car rolls on without you
- Driving (your car): a tire model after GTA V's handling.meta — per-car traction curves (peak grip at ~9° of slip easing to a lower sliding grip), front/rear grip bias, weight transfer, friction circles, traction control, speed-sensitive steering lock and stability damping. Drift: hold gas + brake while turning; the rear steps out and the stick sets the drift angle (~15° light, ~35° full lock); let go and it eases back to grip in about half a second. Burnouts with gas + brake from a stop (steer for donuts)
- Helicopter footage: when the police helicopter is up and near a big incident (shootings, police killings, explosions, arrests), the phone news story gets a 640×360 clip from its belly camera. Scroll stories with the mouse wheel or right stick; tap a story (D-pad → opens the phone; D-pad ↑/↓ to pick, ✕ to play)
- Gang turf: three gangs (Southside Kings, Green Street, East Side Vipers) hold blocks, tinted and outlined on the radar; contested blocks flash. The location line shows whose turf you're on
- Gang members carry different guns: pistols (most common), SMGs, pump shotguns (close in, hit hard up close) and assault rifles (long range); they drop matching ammo
- Bodies sink away about 25 seconds after death
- Cash on bodies: dead civilians often drop a little ($5–60, about 6 in 10), gang members usually drop more ($40–300, about 9 in 10); get out and walk over the wad to grab it (running someone over at ~20 mph+ kills them; their cash drops on the road). Cops and your own crew drop nothing
- Stealing a gang's car sets off its members within ~90 m: they open fire, and up to two carloads run to their other cars and chase you (drive-by shooting from the windows; they pull up and pile out if you stop; they give up after ~90 s or once they lose you, and drive home). Chasers show on the radar in the gang's color. Your own gang's cars are free to take
- Gangs on the map: each gang has a home base (a crowd of members and several cars in its colors), small groups posted on its other blocks (groups of 3 have a car), and a crew of 4 with two cars hanging out nearby. Gangs recruit back up to strength at their home base over time
- Turf wars (src/turf.js): kill 3 of a gang's members on their block to start a war, then wipe out every wave of defenders to take it. Waves are the gang's real members — whoever is nearby first, then crews from posts and the home base, driving over in gang cars when it's far (no one spawns out of nowhere; a gang that runs out of members runs out of waves). Waves scale with the defending gang's strength; home blocks get extra, bigger waves and taking one takes all that gang's turf (the gang goes underground for 24 h). Unclaimed blocks have to be earned (src/claim.js): you need enough street cred (Hustler for your first new block, then a tier up every few blocks held), then hold E on the block to start a 12-minute claim: 1) spray Green Street tags on 3 marked walls (hold E 3 s each), 2) shake down the 2 shop owners out front (hold E to lean on them; some refuse — punch them and they pay but call the cops, or aim a gun at them and they pay quietly), 3) survive the nearest rival gang's response (2–3 waves of their real members). Go down, get busted, or stay off the block 20 s and it fails (that block cools off 3 min). Your tags stay on the walls while you hold the block. `CFG.CLAIM_IGNORE_CRED` skips the cred check for testing. Blocks lock for 2 h after changing hands. NPC gangs roll to attack every 45 min (1 in 3), only one NPC-started war at a time with 30 quiet minutes after each, going after lost blocks first, otherwise by leader personality; every war is fought out live wherever it is. Leave the block for 20 s or die and your attack fails. Turf and timers are saved. `CFG.TURF_FAST` (on while testing) shortens the timers to minutes
- Police and gang wars: police stay out of gang wars (gang-on-gang shooting on gang turf isn't a police matter) and send a unit to investigate afterwards. If the President orders a crackdown on a gang (pause menu stand-in until the President role exists), police and SWAT roll in during that gang's wars and fight its soldiers, and your own turf-war shooting counts as crime if the crackdown is on Green Street. NPC attacking waves are about 40% smaller than defending waves
- Police search: they comb the area where they last saw you (behind cover, around corners), hear gunshots and running footsteps, and keep tracking you if they watched you duck. Escape by leaving the search circle unseen or staying hidden long enough
- 4+ stars: more cruisers, SWAT vans with four-man teams in body armor, and a police helicopter: air support gets called in when you hit 4 stars and takes off from the precinct roof 20–30 s later. It tracks you with a searchlight (buildings block its view) and can be shot down; at 5 stars its door gunner opens fire
- Roadblocks at 4+ stars: police cars drive ahead of you and park across the road (spike strips at 5 stars)
- Civilians react to gunfire: some dive flat, some duck in place, some run to cover and hide, the rest run
- NPC traffic that follows lanes and stops for cars and people
- NPC pedestrians who stay on the sidewalks and cross only at the zebra crosswalks by each intersection (even when running from violence or walking off after a deal), and flee from violence. The exceptions are people diving for cover, people getting out of a car, and a buyer you wave over
- Crimes: assault, carjacking, hit-and-run, store robbery, assaulting officers, stealing police cars
- Wanted level (1–5 stars) with police cruisers dispatched from the station that chase on the road grid, officers who chase on foot, and arrests
- Roadblocks at 2+ stars: two cruisers park across the road ahead of where you're driving, officers guard them, and ramming them is a real crash
- Tires can be shot out: cops at roadblocks aim for your tires as you drive at them; flats sag, spark, slow the car and pull it to that side. You can shoot out other cars' tires too
- Every car has hinged doors that open when people get in and out
- Escaping: break line of sight and police search where you were last seen
- Getting busted: fine, lost street cred, respawn at the police station
- Chop shop: sell stolen cars for cash once the police lose you
- Street cred ladder from the design doc (Nobody → Kingpin)
- Headlines are split across two phone apps, each with its own unread badge and notification: City News (politics, the budget and taxes, the housing market, business, elections) and Crime Reports (shootings, robberies, arrests, raids, gangs, the drug trade). Gang bases moving don't make the news.
- HUD: cash, wanted stars, a round radar that rotates with the player, and an in-game phone with a home screen of apps — Messages (texts from your homies: turf payouts, product sales, stash warnings; and from the plugs: payment reminders that get more threatening as the deadline nears), Contacts (plugs: pickups and paying back), City News, and Missions (what you're in the middle of plus jobs to go do; ✕ sets a waypoint) — and City News (one story per incident: a shootout or rampage gets one headline when it starts and one wrap-up with the death toll when you escape, get busted or go down — not a story per kill; roadblock, spike, helicopter and SWAT news once per chase); a full-screen map (touchpad / M) that pauses the game, shows turf, landmarks, cops and the block under the cursor, and lets you drop a waypoint that shows on the radar with a yellow GPS route along the streets (fewest turns), also drawn on the road in 3D as a see-through strip floating just above it, with sliding chevrons and a light beam on the waypoint; the city stays centered when zoomed out. Hovering a marker on the full map (mouse, or the controller's cursor) — or a row in the legend — makes it grow and shows a label saying what it is. The map's legend (open/close with L / △ or the tab in the corner; remembered) lists everything on it (you, the waypoint, each gang's territory, unclaimed blocks, turf wars, hangouts, police, hospital, chop shop, gun shop, garage, safehouse, stash, plugs, stores, claim spots, police units, the helicopter); the helicopter shows on the radar and the map as a little helicopter pointing where it's flying, with its rotor spinning, lights flashing and its searchlight spot on the ground (brighter when it has you); D-pad ↑↓ / arrow keys step through it (or click a row) and the cursor jumps to that place
- PS5 / standard gamepad support, with button prompts that switch to the controller
- Guns: fists, pistol and SMG with ammo, reloading, aim mode and controller aim assist that locks to the torso (flick up for the head; flick the right stick left or right to snap to the next opp or civilian that way on screen; hold it to one side to break the lock)
- Testing switches in `src/config.js`: `INFINITE_AMMO`, `START_IN_CAR`, `CAR_SPAWN_KEY`
- Rival gang (Southside Kings) on a corner in the Projects who shoot back
- Calling for backup (src/backup.js; phone → Contacts, at the top): your people pile into a car (crew green) and drive over flat out through the streets (slowing only for turns, ~50 mph on the straights), slam to a stop next to you, re-routing if you move (a B on the radar and map, and a BACKUP banner with how far out they are), jump out and fight whoever you're fighting — rival gangs, or the cops once it's a shootout. After a couple of quiet minutes they walk back to the car and drive off. Homies: free every 3 minutes, 2 of them (3 once your base is tier 2–3, 4 at tier 4), carrying whatever crew guns you've bought. Heavy squad: 4 shooters with rifles and vests in a van, $1,500 (bank, then cash), every 5 minutes, from Hustler street cred. A "Call a player" row is there for when multiplayer lets your crewmates answer.
- Gang home bases (src/bases.js): each gang's base is its home block, sized by its strength (blocks held, members, kingpin alive, money in the base; your crew: blocks, homies, turf stash and street cred). Tier 1 trap house (a flag, boarded windows), 2 apartment spot / duplex (+ a banner with the gang's name), 3 warehouse / nightclub (+ neon), 4 penthouse / mansion (+ a flag on the roof). Bigger bases have more guards (4 / 7 / 10 / 13) and cars. What a block can be depends on its district (the Projects and Midtown top out at 2, the Docks and the Strip at 3, Downtown, the Financial District and the Heights at 4); a gang moves to the best site on its own turf once its strength has held for a couple of minutes, only while you're well away, and its kingpin and home crew move with it (City News reports it). Rival bases bank money every game day (their blocks and protection rackets): hold E / ✕ at the door to rob it — the gang comes for you, and their beef with you jumps. Taking the base block in a turf war still wipes the gang off the map. Your crew's base upgrades in place at your hangout. The map shows each base with its tier (legend: strength, tier, stash)
- Gang AI with "heart": low-heart fighters run, mid-heart fighters take cover (building corners, crates, parked cars) and pop out to shoot, reckless ones charge in the open; nerve drops when friends go down, and police in the fight make everyone likelier to run
- Your crew: recruit up to 3 homies at the park hangout (G on the radar); they follow you, ride in your car and fight with the same personality system
- Police open fire at 3+ stars or once you shoot at them, taking cover behind their open car doors, the far side of the cruiser or the hood depending on where you are; a few flank
- Health, damage, "wasted" and hospital respawn
- Turf income (src/shops.js): every block Green Street holds pays every 10 minutes of play, scaled by your street cred tier ($100 a block as Nobody, $150 Hustler, $200 Soldier, $275 Enforcer, $375 Shot Caller, $500 Kingpin; home block pays double; 2 min with `CFG.TURF_FAST`) into a stash on the bench at the hangout ($ on the radar). Walk up and press E to grab it. Two payouts sitting there brings a warning; the third gets the stash hit by the biggest rival gang (they take half)
- Gun shop, Iron Mike's Guns (A on the radar): body armor ($500), ammo, SMG / Pump Shotgun / Assault Rifle, and crew gear (SMGs & shotguns, then rifles for every homie; vests so they take 40% less damage). Won't serve you with stars. You start with fists and a pistol (`CFG.START_ALL_GUNS` gives everything for testing); what you buy is saved
- Body armor: a blue bar next to health that soaks up 70% of each hit until it's worn through
- Mod garage, Lou's Mod Garage (M on the radar): stop the car in the ring and press E. Repair, engine / brakes / armor in three levels each, and respray (with stars: drops one; at 1 star the cops lose you). Mods stay on that car
- Drug dealing (src/drugs.js): three plugs (D on the radar) — Smokey (weed), Doc (pills), Marco (coke). Open the phone and press R / □ for Contacts to set up a pickup: a small pickup, a re-up, or, at Kingpin, a gang run (big load; `CFG.GANG_RUNS_ANY_RANK` unlocks it for testing). Drive to the plug, get out and walk up to them (a ring marks them), press E / D-pad ↑ and wait a few seconds for the handoff, then get it back to the hangout with no cops on you. Risk grows with the product and the load: the cops can raid the spot during the handoff (2–3 stars): a short cinematic — letterbox bars, a flashing RAID title, the camera swinging (at most half a lap, about 4.5 s) and rising into a high, wide-angle shot over the street — as 3–4 cruisers come screaming in from both ends of the road, in both lanes, each pulling up at its own spot 10–15 m from you (clear of your car and anything else in the way) before officers pile out, or the nearest rival gang sends real crews in their cars to chase you down on the way back (gang runs can get both). The plug fronts it, and you pay in person: walk up to the plug with the cash on you and press E / ✕ (Contacts → "Pay back (in person)" sets a waypoint). Three load sizes: a small pickup is the safest but the plug charges the most per unit (weed ~44% margin, pills/coke ~46%); a re-up is bigger, cheaper per unit (~60%) and riskier; a gang run (Kingpin) is the biggest and cheapest (~70%) and riskiest. You have 30 minutes. Late, the plug gives one 10-minute extension with interest: +20% if it wasn't your fault (raid, bust, robbed, killed, market crashed in your district), +50% if it was (you had the money on you or in the stash, or sat on the product for 10+ minutes without selling). Miss that too and there's a hit on you (+50%, shooters in a black car from the plug's corner every 2.5 minutes until you pay). After you die or get busted, the shooters fall back for 5 minutes. Short on cash: Contacts → "Ask for work" gives a job (deliver a package across town in 4 minutes, or collect from someone who runs when he sees you; knock him down) that takes ~40% off the debt, and the shooters hold off while you're on it. Trust per plug: paying on time +8; late −12 (bad luck) or −25 (your fault); a hit −20; a failed job −5. Low trust means higher prices; below 20 the plug wants cash up front at the pickup; below 10 there's no extension. Pushing it: in the last quarter of the clock (or once you're late), Contacts → "Push it" sells cheap (10% off, sliding to 35% off) and buyers come faster and buy more — but never below what you paid per unit. Gang runs split 40% to you, 60% to your homies, who sell it off into the turf stash over the next three payouts. Busted: the cops take everything you're holding
- Drug market (src/market.js): supply and demand per district (Downtown, Financial District, Government District, The Heights, Midtown, The Projects, The Strip, The Docks) and per drug. Each district has its own taste (the Financial District and the Heights pay for coke, the Projects smoke weed, the Strip likes pills). Price = base × demand ÷ supply (0.5×–2.2×). Selling a lot in one place taps buyers out; random events shift demand (party weekend, clinic shut down, dispensary opens). Supply is citywide per drug (the plugs) plus local (rival gangs dealing on their turf): raids cut a drug's supply and make plugs charge more; plugs can be killed (that drug dries up until a new plug takes over, inheriting your tab); each rival gang has a kingpin (♛) at its home base, and killing one dries up their corners; your big runs flood the area and push prices down. Everything drifts back to normal over several minutes. `CFG.MARKET_HOT` (on while testing) makes the market hot: demand 1.5× everywhere, demand events every ~45 s, buyers 3× as often A drive-up buyer stuck in traffic nearby gets out and walks over. Buyers come more often where demand for what you hold is high and want what's popular there. Phone → Market app shows every district's price for each drug with ▲▼ trends and why (✕ sets a waypoint); on the full map, H / □ cycles a price heat map (blue cheap → red high) with district prices
- President's approval rating (src/president.js, pause menu → President's orders): driven by a crime level that every crime raises (weighted: a cop killed counts far more than a carjacking; NPC turf wars count by their body count; drug killings count) and that fades over ~10 minutes. Approval drifts toward 65% in a quiet city and falls as crime climbs; arrests, drug raids and street sweeps help, suspects escaping hurts. Crackdowns and the war on drugs read as strong when crime is high and heavy-handed when it's quiet. Big swings make City News; the panel shows the rating, the crime level and what's driving it
- War on drugs (pause menu → President's orders): Hands off / Tough on drugs / All-out war. Each step scares off buyers (demand −15%), seizes supply (−20%, so prices and plug costs climb), makes raids on pickups likelier and puts more undercover cops among buyers; all-out war adds street sweeps that clear a district's corners every few minutes
- Selling on rival turf (src/turfrisk.js): their buyers are loyal (pay 25% less, come less often) unless that gang's kingpin is dead (then their buyers come to you and pay 25% more). Each sale can get you noticed — more near their people, on their home block, and the more you sell on one block: first a warning text, then (within 3 minutes) their crew opens fire or drives over to find you. Sometimes one of their soldiers walks up for a 30% cut of what you made there: pay (E / ✕) and you can sell there for 5 minutes, refuse (Q / ○) and they come at you — drop and they take everything you're holding. Selling there builds beef with that gang (cools off over time): a warning text at 50, and at 100 they hit your stash (or one of your blocks if it's empty)
- After a deal buyers head back the way they came and out of sight; one who parked and walked over gets back in their car and drives off. Undercover cops don't walk away: badge out, gun on you, a short "POLICE" cinematic, and backup cruisers that were already waiting nearby race in from both ends of the street within a couple of seconds, jump out and aim from behind their doors. They hold fire: stand still for a few seconds and you're arrested (fine, product seized); run off or pull a gun and they open fire. Raids work the same way. What you carry is at risk: killed by opps (a rival gang), they take all your cash and product; killed by the plug's shooters, they take all your cash (it comes off what you owe) and the product; killed by the cops, they seize the product; any other death (a crash, a fire) drops the product where you went down (gone after 90 s). Busted: a fine and all your product. Dirty cops: a patrol stop at 1–2 stars (not a raid or a sting) can be a dirty cop — 20% of the time, more at night (10 PM–4 AM), most in Southgate and Harbor. Each stop shows 1–2 details about the officer; dirty ones slip (came alone, no siren, unmarked car, "what you got on you?", waves you around the corner, hasn't cuffed you, or a name you've seen in City News misconduct stories) but not always, and straight cops sometimes come alone. E / ✕ go quietly: a straight cop arrests you; a dirty cop takes everything (cash and product) and lets you walk, wanted level cleared. Q / ○ offer a bribe: a dirty cop takes your product and half your cash; a straight cop adds a bribery charge (bigger fine, −25 cred). `CFG.TEST_DIRTY_COP` forces 'dirty' or 'clean'
- Selling: while you're holding and not on a run or wanted, buyers walk up (or drive up and pull over at the curb). A buyer across the street walks to the curb on their side and waves you down: E / ✕ waves them over and they cut straight across; Q / ○ or ignoring them for 12 s sends them on their way. Buyers on foot come to whichever car window is closer, usually the curb side. E / ✕ sells, Q / ○ turns them down. Some are undercover cops (2 stars; sometimes you get a tell, like "shoes are too clean"); some snatch it and run (knock them down to get it back). Higher street cred brings buyers more often (every 90 s as a Nobody down to 25 s as Kingpin), bigger buys, better prices and fewer robberies
- Safehouse (S on the radar): $5,000. You respawn there after being wasted or busted, start there, and can rest to heal and save
- Cars take damage, catch fire and explode; ammo drops from downed enemies

- Dialogue voice: homies, plugs and street buyers talk in African American Vernacular English, written from research (kept outside the repo) — grammar over respelling, light slang, coded product talk, short calm threats; each plug has their own voice (Smokey laid back, Doc clipped and cold, Marco an older OG), homies text warm, buyers sound anxious, and undercover cops sometimes give themselves away by naming the drug like a cop would. Lines live in `VOICES` / `ASKS` in src/drugs.js and the homie texts in src/shops.js. No slurs and no real-gang vocabulary

## Playing on a phone

Open the game link on your phone and turn it sideways. Touch controls switch on at the first touch (src/touch.js); they work like a virtual PS5 controller, so every prompt, menu and the phone work the same way.
- Left thumb: a joystick that appears wherever you touch the left side. On foot it moves you (push it all the way out to sprint); in a car it steers, and up/down is gas/brake.
- Right thumb: drag anywhere on the right side to look around. Drag off FIRE to aim while shooting. AIM is a toggle (tap on, tap off) and gets the controller's aim assist.
- On foot: FIRE, AIM, ✕ (yes / sprint), ○ (punch / no), □ reload, △ get in a car, DUCK, GUN (tap to swap weapons, hold for the inventory strip, tap again to close it) and USE, which lights up yellow when there's something to do.
- Driving: GAS, BRAKE, HAND BRAKE, △ exit, ✕ / ○ for prompts (tickets, deals), USE.
- Top row: pause, 📱 phone, MAP, CAR (test car while that testing switch is on).
- Phone, shop menus and the inventory strip: a D-pad plus ✕ / ○ on the left. Rows can be tapped too: the first tap selects (so you can read the details), the second buys or opens.
- Map: drag to pan (when zoomed in), pinch to zoom, tap to drop a waypoint; buttons for zoom, legend, heat map and close.
- Phones get "Phone" graphics automatically (src/quality.js): lower render resolution, no antialiasing, cheaper and shorter shadows, a shorter view distance (fog closes in sooner), 60% of the people, 70% of the traffic and fewer parked cars drawn far away. Switch between Phone and Full in the pause menu (saved), or add `?quality=phone` / `?quality=full` to the address. `?touch` forces the touch controls on.
- The HUD, phone, shops, map, pause menu and title screen are laid out for small landscape screens; in portrait you're asked to turn the phone. Android goes full screen when you tap PLAY (on iPhone, use Share → Add to Home Screen for full screen).
